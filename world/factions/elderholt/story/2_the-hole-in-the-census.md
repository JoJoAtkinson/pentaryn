# The Hole in the Census

**A two-player one-shot, D&D 5.5e, level 9, set in 3948 A.F.**

> **This is a placeholder.** It records what has been decided and what has not. No scenes,
> no encounters, no stat blocks yet. The title is provisional.

Marrith Ashcroft — three hundred and forty years before anyone calls her *the Ashen
Measure* — goes looking for a woman who cannot be found, and gets filed instead.

## Decided

### Why the coven needs her

[[kharbora-the-living-shadow\|Kharbora]] can kill matron mothers. [[taramet-the-forge\|Taramet]]
can forge a door. Nobody in Elderholt knows *where the corridor goes* or how to lock a
thing so it stays locked. The Wayward Witch does.

This also closes a loose end already in the vault: Taramet forged the door and **lost the
key, and isn't worried about it.** Because the key was never hers. In dwarven lands the
Wayward Witch is Barthram-dur, who carries the **Ring of Borrowed Keys** — *opens any door,
but none work twice in the same place.* Losing it costs nothing. It was never going to work
twice.

And she cannot be summoned: no fixed name, no fixed form, no Sendings, never three days in
one place.

### Why they send a nobody

**The Elders physically cannot leave.** [[silniel-witch-tree\|Silniel]] is a tree rooted in
Elderstead. [[calyrix-the-many-minded\|Calyrix]] is shattered across doorframes and mirrors
inside the city. [[raven-the-vital-ascendant\|Raven]] is a lich. Kharbora is a living
shadow. Elderholt's power is also its immobility.

So the errand goes to someone unmarked and un-monikered: a young ward-witch who has never
killed anything. The unspoken second reason — Kharbora kills, Taramet builds, and nobody
had asked *who decides where the line falls.* She thinks she is running an errand. She is
being auditioned.

### Why Calderon, and how they are taken

The Wayward Witch leaves no footprints — she leaves **a gap in a ledger.** The
[[world/factions/calderon-imperium/_overview\|Calderon Imperium]] writes down everything;
it is the one realm on the continent where someone unrecordable shows up as an absence.

So Marrith is not storming a palace. She is breaking into **a tax office** to steal a
filing discrepancy.

**They are not ambushed. They are processed.** The Censorate noticed the gap eleven years
ago — in the file it is an *unregistered persistent anomaly* — and has been baiting whoever
comes looking for it ever since. They are taken because they *succeed*.

Then the Imperium does the worst thing it knows how to do: it **registers her.** Once she
is on the rolls she is a resident, the Edict Code applies, and everything done to her
afterward is procedurally correct. Her docketed crimes are real and boring — unregistered
arcane practice, entry without permit, tampering with a Censorate archive. She is not a
heretic. She is a case number.

### Hollowbank

A sealed facility with a civil name and a file name. Civil: **Hollowbank**. File:
**Registry Station Four**. Locals think it is a revenue office. (Not
[[ironstead\|Ironstead]] — this is provincial and boring on purpose.)

**Guilt-free opposition, by design:** the staff are **clerks discharged from the registry**
— struck from the rolls, legally dead, still employed. They still stamp. They still file.
They will kill you and log it. Above them, licensed battlemages and augur-clerks who
volunteered.

### The elf

Male, level 9, **rogue/druid**, from the southern continent. Alive in the present day.

- **Not the crow.** [[04327-07-00_marwen-007\|Marrith's crow]] stays a separate thing.
- He came east **on an Imperium survey ship** — not an explorer, a return trip, following
  the people who came to his home.
- **Why he wants her too:** the Wayward Witch has a guise for every culture — human, orc,
  dwarf, elf, Elderholt. **She has none for his people.** He wants to know whether she has
  never come, or came and they don't remember.
- **The second-act turn:** Calderon has already surveyed and filed his homeland. He is not
  a trespasser, he is a specimen from an unregistered territory — and the only map of his
  home is in a folder in the same drawer as hers, in a building about to be on fire.
- They are not friends. They are a merger.
- **Name candidates:** Torvael, Naenwyr, Ilvareth. Must read elven but *not* Araethilene —
  no `-iel`/`-ith` melisma. His real name is long; among humans he answers to one syllable.

### The southern continent

Off every map. Merrowgate's cartographers put a compass rose over it; Ardenhaven draws sea;
**Calderon's Censorate shows a blank with a registry note.**

Imperium survey voyages ran quietly for ~20 years up to 3948. They found it, surveyed it,
filed it — and then the Age of Conquest ate the next 327 years of imperial attention.
Nobody went back. **It is blank through bureaucratic neglect, not conspiracy.**

- **Two names needed.** A mainland *description* by people who have never been, and the
  real endonym in a naming system not yet invented. The second can wait.
- **The naming hook:** the proto-root `ar` — home, foundation, origin — is the fingerprint
  of Ao Sathanum. **Southern words do not carry it anywhere.** Either they predate the
  ancient empire or the Fall never reached them. A Merrowgate linguist notices and does not
  know what to do with it. Never has to be resolved.
- **The druids live here.** *The Unthreaded* — people cut off from whatever was feeding
  them, who learned to take from the land raw. This replaces the earlier mainland-mountaintop
  version and answers "why has nobody mentioned druids" with geography.

### What she brings home — none of it the witch

1. **The correction.** You do not *find* Elder Way; you become worth arriving for. She
   spends a year in a filing cabinet chasing someone who was always going to walk into
   Elderstead on her own. Two years later, she does.
2. **Elderholt is in the ledger.** Headcounts, road crossings, tithe estimates, the names
   of the villages that pay. The coven believes itself unfindable. It is *catalogued*, by a
   bureaucracy that does not think this is remarkable.
3. **Herself.** She went in having never killed. She got out through a building staffed by
   people struck from the rolls, deciding in real time and repeatedly exactly where the
   fire stopped and who was inside it.

> **This relocates her title.** She does not earn *the Ashen Measure* judging Elwen in
> [[04288-00-00_elder-005\|4288]]. She earns it at Hollowbank in 3949. The Elwen judgment
> is the day everyone else finally understood what the name had always meant — 339 years of
> people mistaking her restraint for gentleness.

The reward is one line, off-screen, in a history file: **3950, Kharbora — who has never
once asked a young witch's opinion about anything — asks Marrith where the line should
fall.**

### The last beat

They get out. Dawn, a road, filthy, nothing left. A woman in dust-stained boots and too
many rings passes them going the other way, and nods.

And keeps walking.

They do not recognise her. **The players will.** No dialogue. She was never findable — she
was also never avoiding them.

### Marrith's build

**Evoker Wizard 9. Straight, no multiclass.** The subclass is the character:

- **Sculpt Spells** — carve allies *out of your own Fireball*, zero damage, automatic. This
  is *"flame that did not spread"* printed as a class feature.
- **Potent Cantrip** — half on a save. She does not miss; she decides how much.
- **Wall of Fire (4th)**, ring option, damage on **one side only** — the Elwen execution,
  340 years early. Her signature.
- Fire that is all *shape*: Burning Hands, Scorching Ray, Flaming Sphere, Continual Flame,
  Fire Bolt, Fire Shield. Ward-witch half off the same list: Alarm, Shield, Mage Armor,
  Arcane Lock, Glyph of Warding, Magic Circle, Remove Curse, Dispel Magic, Counterspell.
- At 9: 4/3/3/3/1 slots, 14 prepared, 4 cantrips.

**Not sorcerer.** Careful Spell is Sculpt Spells but worse and costs a point per cast.
Draconic red adds *more* fire; her whole character is *less* fire, exactly placed. Wrong
axis.

**Optional flavour button, zero power gain:**

> **The Measure.** Before rolling a fire spell's damage, Marrith may drop any number of its
> dice. For each die dropped, the spell's edge becomes a hard boundary the fire will not
> cross, and it ignites nothing.

## Open — needs a decision

1. **The southern continent's name.** Pinned deliberately; Joe is thinking.
2. **How long are they held?** Working assumption ~14 months — long enough that Elderholt
   has written her off, short enough that she is home before the Purge. Not settled.
3. **Has the elf ever heard of Elderholt?** If *no* — if he is the one person on the
   continent with no opinion about witches — that is the most interesting thing about him
   in the cell.
4. **His name.** Torvael / Naenwyr / Ilvareth, or something else.

## Considered and rejected

Recorded so they don't get re-litigated:

- **Araethilion as captor** — the elves keep the only sealed ledger of the Wayward Witch's
  movements, because a nameless woman disproves the Silver Thread; the prison is
  *hospitality*, no cell, just a name and a thread. Good, but Calderon gives a cleaner
  conscience about killing, and the elf's origin moved off-continent.
- **The elf as Marrith's crow** — his escape costs his last wildshape and he is still with
  her family 377 years later. Strong, but it spends a live-campaign mystery.
- **The elf as a Severed Araethilene** — died with the elven-captor version.
- **Druids on mainland mountaintops** — replaced by the southern continent, which answers
  their absence better.
- **The Wayward Witch imprisoned in Hollowbank**, or freeing them herself — both make the
  Imperium good enough to hold her, which is too large a claim.
- **A west-coast frontier** instead of a southern continent — you can walk west; that is
  exactly why it fails to explain a blank map.
