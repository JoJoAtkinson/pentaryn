# A Quiet Lesson — 12 Switchback Steps

> Nonlethal only. Leave him breathing. No maiming.

**Where:** the Switchback Steps, **Middle Tier**, off Western Market Lane — a stone stair that climbs the tier wall in four flights and three landings. Number 12 is the tenement at the second landing. Quest: **Quiet Lesson — Clean-Street Work**, [writ board (CR2)](../../../quests/writ-board-cr2/_board.md). 50 gp per hand, payment left with the barkeep. No employer's name anywhere on the paper.
**Tuned for:** 4–6 level-1 PCs. Same map, same statblocks, **200 XP** in every variant — pick one at the table and swap without re-tuning.

| | Variant | What it is | XP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **v1** | [The Lesson](#v1--the-lesson) | The straight fight. Nonlethal both ways, no complications. | 200 |
| **v2** | [The Neighbours](#v2--the-neighbours) | Wrong people defending him, for the right reason. The fight becomes optional. | 200 |
| **v3** | [Veska on the Landing](#v3--veska-on-the-landing) | A named Gray District woman negotiating mid-fight, terrified, not moving. | 200 |

All three use **bandit ×4** (25 XP each) + **tough ×1** (100 XP) = **200 XP**. Only the *reason* changes.

## Shared: the rule, and why it cuts both ways

**Read this out loud before initiative, in every variant. It is the spine of the encounter.**

The party is under a **nonlethal-only** contract: leave Calderwick breathing, no maiming. Fine — that's a constraint on the players, and players are used to constraints.

**The people on the stair are under the same rule.** Different reasons in each variant, same iron result: this is a Middle Tier stair with forty open shutters looking down onto it. A body brings the city watch. The watch brings questions. Nobody standing on these steps — nobody — wants their name written down by anyone. They will beat six adventurers unconscious and roll them down four flights before they will let one of them die on their landing.

So:

- **Every NPC here attacks nonlethally by default.** `club` for the hands, `mace` and `pommel_and_boot` for the leader. A PC dropped to 0 HP by these is **unconscious and stable** — no death saves, no dying, no maiming. They wake an hour later at the bottom of the stair with their purse still on them and their weapons stacked beside them.
- **The moment a PC kills someone, the rule dies for everybody.** The hands drop the clubs and draw the `scimitar`. This is not a morale shift, it is a category change: the thing they were protecting is already gone, and now they are only fighting. That is when this encounter gets someone killed.
- **A TPK here is not a TPK.** It is a beating, and then a very awkward morning.

That asymmetry is the fight. **The party can lose without dying, and can only really lose by winning wrong.**

## Shared: terrain

- **The stair:** four flights, roughly 5 ft wide, worn concave by two centuries of feet. **No room to flank.** Two Medium creatures abreast is a squeeze; three is not happening. Every fight on a flight is a front-rank fight — the party's back rank is spectating and the front rank is taking everything.
- **The landings:** three of them, about 15 × 10 ft. The only places a battle line can form. Number 12's door opens onto the second landing. Whoever holds a landing controls the stair above and below it.
- **Loose footing:** treads wet most evenings, slick with wash-water always. A creature that Dashes, is Pushed, or is knocked Prone **on a flight** (not a landing) **tumbles 1d4 × 5 ft downhill** and takes **1d6 bludgeoning**. This applies to NPCs too — shoving people down stairs is nonlethal, efficient, and exactly in the spirit of the contract.
- **Washing lines:** rope strung between the tenement faces at head height, hung with sheets. Total cover behind a hung sheet, and it moves. Cut a line and the sheets drop onto the flight below (difficult terrain, 1 round); grab one and swing (DC 12 Acrobatics, and say yes).
- **Light:** one oil lamp on a bracket per landing. **Dim** on the flights, bright on the landings. Break the lamps and the whole stair is dark — nobody here has darkvision, and everybody is on wet stone in a 5-ft corridor.
- **Windows:** packed stone tenements, three and four storeys, shutters open in warm weather. There are people watching from every one of them, and in **v2** and **v3** not one of them will shout for the watch. That silence is the district.

## Shared: how they fight

Nobody here is a tactician. They fight in a **plug**: two hands hold the flight above the landing, two hold the flight below, the leader stands on the landing itself, adjacent to whichever side is losing. **Pack Tactics is live nearly every round** because there is nowhere to stand that isn't next to somebody.

- **They do not pursue off the stair.** A party that withdraws to Western Market Lane is not followed.
- **They do not go for downed PCs.** Once a PC is at 0 and stable they step over them. `pommel_and_boot` is for a PC who is prone or grappled and *still fighting* — it ends a fight, it doesn't finish a person.
- **The leader calls it off** at half HP, or when two of theirs are down, or the instant a credible offer is made (`call_it_off`). The only one who can end it for everybody.
- **Individual hands break on their own** at half HP or once the leader drops (`throw_down_arms`) — but a hand that surrenders and is *then* attacked ends the nonlethal rule for the whole stair.

# v1 — The Lesson

**The straight fight. 200 XP.** Use this when the group wants a clean tactical problem and an interesting constraint, and doesn't want to be made to feel anything about it.

**Ryn Calderwick** owes somebody money. He is a barrel-maker at 12 Switchback Steps, mid-forties, flour-pale and thin, with a Calderon accent he has spent four years failing to sand off. He rents his upper floor to four lodgers and he drinks with the man at number 14, and when strangers come up the stair with the look of people on a writ, all five of them come out. Not out of love. Out of the arithmetic of living in a building where the stair is the only way in or out.

**Calderwick is not a combatant.** No stat block. He does not fight and he does not run — he stands in his doorway and asks who sent them. Answer honestly and he goes grey, because the honest answer is *nobody put a name on the paper*.

**Opening:** the party knocks, or doesn't. They get one round with Calderwick in the doorway, and then doors open above and below and the stair fills. The party is surrounded on a staircase with no room to flank before a blow is struck. The **tough** comes out of number 14 last and asks them to leave. Once.

**Outcome:** beat five people badly enough to get past them to a man standing behind them who never raised a hand, then collect 50 gp a head from a barkeep. The contract is satisfied. Nobody dies. Nobody feels good, but nobody is *supposed* to feel anything in v1 — that's what v2 is for.

# v2 — The Neighbours

**The moral axis. Same 200 XP, same statblocks, different people holding the clubs.** Use this when the group has been fighting things for a while and needs the ground to shift under them.

**The four are not Calderwick's lodgers and they are not muscle.** They are a joiner, two dock-hands, and a woman who bakes. They live on this stair. They came out of their own doors carrying whatever was beside the door, and none of them wanted this, and all four of them are here.

Because **Calderwick is a Gray District man**, and *a Calderon refugee being jumped on a stair by strangers* is the precise shape of the thing every one of them left the Imperium to get away from. Nobody on this stair has to have it explained. They saw the party coming up the flights and they knew what it was before the party opened its mouth.

**What that changes mechanically:**

- **The fight is optional, and it is partial.** The neighbours are not trying to win. They are trying to make this take long enough, and cost enough, that whoever bought it decides it was a bad purchase. A party that fights its way to the second landing and stops has *already* done the job the writ asked for.
- **Calderwick will take it himself.** If the fight goes two rounds, he steps out onto the landing and offers a straight trade: *hit me, leave them, get your money.* He means it. He will stand there and let them. **[ASK PLAYER]** nothing — there is no check, he just does it. A party that accepts gets paid in full and gets to watch four neighbours be unable to stop it.
- **The neighbours can be talked past.** Not talked *down* — past. **[ASK PLAYER] DC 13 Persuasion or Insight.** Success doesn't make them friendly; it makes them step aside, because the party has said something that proves they understand what they're standing in. They will not help, they will not thank anyone, and they will watch.
- **Retreat costs nothing and gains nothing.** The party can walk down the stair at any point. The writ goes unpaid. Nobody follows.

**The ugly part, said plainly:** somebody paid Ardenford rates to have a Gray District man beaten in public by strangers with no connection to the district. Every neighbour on the stair has recognised the shape of it and none of them will say it out loud. **That is how the Imperium tells you it knows where you live.** The party are the message. They may work it out on the walk down, or when the second writ goes up.

**If the party takes the money anyway:** they should. That's the variant working. Let them collect the 50 gp a head with no penalty, no karma system, and no NPC lecture — and then, three sessions later, let a Gray District shopkeeper recognise one of them and quietly not serve them.

# v3 — Veska on the Landing

**The named antagonist. Same 200 XP, same map, built around one voice.** Use this when Joe wants somebody to *play* rather than a fight to run.

Everything in **v2** holds. The difference is that the **tough** has a name, and she talks through the entire fight.

## Veska Torlan

*Former Calderon officer. Number 14, two doors up from Calderwick. Fifty-odd, broad, in a gambeson older than most of the people on the stair.*

She left the Imperium rather than serve another term. That sentence is the whole character: she has already made the one enormous, irreversible, family-condemning decision of her life, and she is now standing on a wet staircase in a foreign country doing the small brave thing instead, because the small brave thing is what is in front of her.

**She is frightened.** Play her frightened. Not shaking — she's a soldier, her hands are fine — but frightened in the specific way of someone who *recognises the procedure*. She has seen this done. She may have done it. She knows a writ with no name on it is not a debt collection, and she worked that out before the party finished climbing the first flight, and she is standing on the landing anyway.

**Voice notes for the table:**

- **She is the only one who speaks.** The neighbours say nothing at all, the entire fight. Just her, at conversational volume, over the sound of it.
- **Short declarative sentences.** She does not plead and she does not threaten. She states positions. *"You can go down."* *"He has not got fifty gold."* *"Who gave you the paper."*
- **She asks the same question three times** across the fight and it gets quieter each time: **who posted the writ.** She is not fishing for the party's employer. She is confirming a thing she already believes and does not want to be right about.
- **She never swears and never raises her voice**, which on a staircase where five people are hitting each other is deeply unsettling.
- **She never steps away from Calderwick's door.** Whatever else she does, that is where she is.

**Set-piece — the mid-fight negotiation.** At the top of **round 3**, or the moment `wade_in` fires and one of hers goes down, Veska calls `call_it_off` *whether or not she has hit a morale step*. She steps into the middle of the landing with her mace hanging at her thigh, in reach of two PCs, and makes the offer with her guard down:

> *"Stop. — Stop. You want the fifty. Take it off me. I have got thirty-one and a ring and you can have both and I will tell the barkeep it is done, and he will believe me, because he knows me and he does not know you. Then you go down the steps and you never come back up them, and I do not write anybody's name anywhere, ever. That is the whole offer. I am not going to make you a better one."*

Then she stands there and waits, in reach, and does not defend herself.

**Resolve it honestly:**

- **Accept:** the encounter is **over**. Award **full 200 XP**. The party gets 31 gp and a silver ring, and the barkeep does pay out — Veska keeps her word and her word is good. They are down 19 gp a head and up an ally in a district that has none.
- **Refuse and keep fighting:** she raises the mace and goes back to work and does not offer again. This is a legitimate outcome. Do not punish it — but she stops talking, and the silence on the stair afterward is worse than the talking was.
- **Attack her while her guard is down:** allow it. Advantage, and she takes it, and she does not go down from one hit. Then she looks at whoever did it for a beat too long and says nothing at all for the rest of the fight. **This is the single most damaging thing the party can do in this encounter** and it costs them no HP.
- **Ask her the question back — *who do you think posted it?*** — and she tells them. Straight, flat, no hedging, standing on the landing: *"Somebody who wanted a Calderon man beaten in the street by people who have never heard of him. That is not a debt. That is a letter."* This is the hook out of the encounter and it is worth more than 50 gp a hand.

## Aftermath (all variants)

Payment is genuinely at the barkeep's: **50 gp per hand**, in a purse, no note. Calderwick is bruised and breathing and does not report it, because reporting things is not what people from the Gray District do.

He is also now a man who has been publicly beaten by strangers on his own landing while his neighbours watched — which is precisely what somebody paid for. Follow the thread: a second writ, a second Gray District name, and a party who now know exactly what they were used for.

## NPCs

- **bandit** ×4 — the hands (CR 1/8 each, 25 XP). Clubs, not blades. In v1 they are Calderwick's lodgers; in v2 and v3 they are his neighbours. Reskin freely: a joiner's mallet, a boat-hook haft, a rolling pin, a length of firewood.
- **tough** ×1 — the leader (CR 1/2, 100 XP). Unnamed in v1; **Veska Torlan** in v2 and v3, played in full in v3.
- **Ryn Calderwick** — the target. No stat block. Unarmed, does not fight, does not run.

**XP: 4 × 25 + 100 = 200 XP in every variant.**
