# Veska Torlan

**HP** 32 (5d8+10) **·** **AC** 12 (a soldier's gambeson, twenty years old, still oiled) **·** **Speed** 30 ft. **·** **Athletics** +2 **·** **Passive Perception** 10 **·** **Languages** Common (Calderon accent she stopped trying to hide) **·** **CR** 1/2 (100 XP)

> Action mechanics live in `combat-runner/actions.jsonl` — see the launcher's **Ready actions** reference for verbs.

**Pack Tactics.** Advantage on an attack roll against a creature if any ally of Veska's is within 5 ft of it and not Incapacitated. **On a 5-ft-wide stair with four of her neighbours on it, this is live nearly every round.** Do not forget it — it is most of why she is dangerous.

**Who she is:** a former Calderon officer who left rather than serve another term. Number 14, two doors up from Calderwick. She is the only person on this stair who understands exactly what the writ actually is — and that is precisely why she is the most frightened person on it.

---

## Per variant (see [`_overview.md`](../_overview.md))

- **v1 — The Lesson:** unnamed. She is *the man from number 14*, Calderwick's drinking companion, a minder in all but pay. Run the block, skip the character. One line of dialogue: she asks them to leave, once.
- **v2 — The Neighbours:** she is Veska, but she barely speaks. She is one of five people who came out of their doors, and she is the one who ends it.
- **v3 — Veska on the Landing:** the full character. She talks through the entire fight and there is a scripted mid-fight offer she makes with her guard down. **Play her frightened** — not shaking, she is a soldier and her hands are fine, but frightened in the specific way of someone who *recognises the procedure*. She has seen this done. Short declarative sentences, never raises her voice, never swears, and asks the same question three times, quieter each time: **who posted the writ.**

---

## Start-of-turn checklist

1. **Pack Tactics:** any ally within 5 ft of her target? → **advantage**. Check the treads before you roll.
2. **Is the rule still on?** Nobody killed → **Mace**, nonlethal. **Pommel & Boot** only against a prone, grappled, or already-downed target. If a PC has killed someone, she stops holding back and everything is lethal from that moment.
3. **Morale clock — has she hit a step?** At or below **16 HP**, *or* two of hers down, *or* a credible offer made → **Call It Off**. Advance the clock one step every time **Wade In** fires.
4. **Wade In** (reaction) refreshes to AVAILABLE.

---

## Tactics — when the DM asks "what does it do?"

- **Before initiative:** she asks them to leave. Once, flatly, without threat. If the DM only runs one line of dialogue in this encounter, run this one — she is giving the party a way out before anyone has rolled anything, and most parties will not take it.
- **Round 1:** she takes **the landing**, not a flight. From the landing she is adjacent to whichever side of the stair is losing, and she can reach either. She does not open the fight; she waits for the party to.
- **Mid-fight:** **Mace** the PC who is doing the most damage to her neighbours, with Pack Tactics advantage. She does not chase a target through the plug — she lets the stair bring them to her.
- **Wade In:** the moment one of hers drops within 15 ft, she moves to them, plants herself between them and whoever did it, and that PC becomes her target next round. This is the only time she moves off the landing.
- **Pommel & Boot:** only on a PC who is prone, grappled, or at 0 HP and still a problem. It is how she *ends* a fight, not how she finishes a person — nonlethal, unconscious and stable, done.
- **She never touches Calderwick's door.** Whatever else happens, she is standing where the stair meets it.
- **At or below 16 HP, or two down:** **Call It Off.** *"That's it. That's enough."* She names terms and she means them — walk down, we never saw you, the writ can say what it likes. She will not offer twice.
- **If a PC kills anyone:** she stops calling it off, stops holding back, and fights to 0 HP. So does everyone still standing. This is the failure state of the encounter and it should feel like a door closing.

## What she actually wants

Not the fight. She wants to know **who posted the writ**, and she has already worked out that the answer is *nobody put a name on it* — which means this was never about a debt. Somebody paid Ardenford rates to have a Gray District man beaten in public by strangers with no connection to the district.

She knows what that is. She has seen it done. A party that stops swinging and asks the right question gets a straight answer from her, which is more than anyone else in the district will give them, and it is worth considerably more than 50 gp a hand.

## Description (one line)

A broad woman in her fifties in a gambeson older than most of the people on the stair, holding a mace low and loose against her thigh, standing on the landing like it is a position she was assigned.
