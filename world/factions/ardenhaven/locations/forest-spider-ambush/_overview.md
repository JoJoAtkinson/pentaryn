# Forest Spider Ambush — Road to the Ruins

> The shortcut runs through old wood. In the last quarter mile the birdsong stopped, and the branches overhead are wrong — too grey, too thick, too still.

**Where:** a wooded hollow on the track between Ardenford and the [[ruins-western-scrubland|Western Scrubland Ruins]] — a roadside fight on the way to the goblin job.
**Tuned for:** 4–6 level-1 PCs. Same hollow, same statblocks, **300 XP** in every variant — pick one on the walk to the table and swap without re-tuning.

| | Variant | What it is | XP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **v1** | [The Shortcut](#v1--the-shortcut) | The straight fight. Web, fangs, no reason and no mercy. | 300 |
| **v2** | [The Cart](#v2--the-cart) | A dead pedlar, a live pony, and a drag-trail that leads back to the lumber camp. | 300 |
| **v3** | [Tam Ackley](#v3--tam-ackley) | A boy runs into the middle of the fight from the direction the party is going. | 300 |

All three use **giant-spider ×1** (200 XP) + **giant-wolf-spider ×2** (50 XP each) = **300 XP**. The picker counts never change. Only the *reason* does.

---

## Read this first — what this encounter is for

**This is a speed bump.** It sits between Ardenford and somewhere the party actually wants to be, and its whole job is to cost them resources, daylight, and a little nerve before the real fight. Every variant below is built to still run in **four to six rounds and twenty minutes**.

So: **do not let a variant become the session.** v2 and v3 each add exactly one thing to care about besides the spiders, and both of them resolve on the road, in the same scene, without a side-trip. If the table starts settling in, kill the last wolf spider early and move them down the track.

**Scaling for six:** add a third giant wolf spider (`count: 3`) → **350 XP**. Do not add a second giant spider — two Webs in one round on a level-1 party is how a speed bump becomes a funeral.

## Shared: terrain

- **The webbed canopy.** A grey sheet-web slung between the crowns, **15–20 ft up**, spanning maybe 60 ft of road. Web squares are **difficult terrain**; a creature that ends up in the canopy without Spider Climb is in a hammock it does not understand. All three spiders ignore all of it (Web Walker).
- **The hollow.** The track dips ten feet over its length and the banks rise on both sides — this is why the web is here. **The banks are difficult terrain** and cost 10 ft of movement to scramble up. The road itself is the only clean ground, which is exactly the point: everything the party wants to do fast has to happen in a 15-ft-wide lane with a ceiling.
- **Forest floor.** Undergrowth, roots, leaf litter deep enough to hide a hound in. **Light difficult terrain** off the track. Both wolf spiders start buried in it, prone and motionless, **Stealth +5** against passive Perception.
- **Trunks and deadfall.** Half cover, climbable for 10 ft of movement, and they break line of sight — the wolf spiders use them to get behind the back rank. All three spiders climb at full speed, upside down, without checks.
- **Light.** Dappled shade under a canopy that is mostly web. **Effectively dim** on the road, properly dark in the deadfall. Nobody rolling Perception here is having a good time.
- **The way out is the road.** Both ways. The forest either side is passable and slow, and a party that leaves the track is a party the wolf spiders' 40 ft speed catches one at a time.

## Shared: how the spiders fight

**No leader, no parley, no retreat, and nothing here can be reasoned with.** Say that to yourself before initiative so you don't accidentally play them as tactically flexible — they aren't. They are a control fight and a grind.

- **Round 1 is a Web** from the canopy at the most dangerous-looking PC — 60 ft, **[ASK PLAYER] DC 13 Dex save**, Restrained on a fail. The web is an object: **AC 10, HP 5, vulnerable to fire, immune to poison and psychic**.
- **Then it drops on whoever is Restrained.** Attacks against a Restrained creature have Advantage, and a `bite` at +5 for 1d8+3 piercing plus **2d6 poison** will drop most level-1 PCs from full in two hits. This is the whole threat. Take it seriously.
- **It owns the vertical.** Between bites the giant spider climbs back to 15 ft, out of shortsword reach. Melee PCs have to climb a trunk, cut the web out from under it, or find a rock. **Reward all three.**
- **The wolf spiders never stand and trade.** Burst out of the litter, bite (+3, 1d4+1 plus **[ASK PLAYER] DC 11 Con save**, 2d4 poison, half on a success), then run — 40 ft, up a trunk, out of reach. Flank when they can; both adjacent to one PC is Advantage for both.
- **They run down stragglers.** Anything fleeing, anything below half, anything that gets separated. If a PC breaks for the road, a wolf spider goes with them and it is faster than they are.
- **Larder rule** (same as the [[world/factions/ardenhaven/locations/old-lumber-camp/_overview|old lumber camp]], and it matters in v2): the instant a creature cuts into a cocoon, a sling, or an egg sac, the **giant spider commits to that creature for the rest of the fight** — walking through opportunity attacks, ignoring everyone else, until one of them is dead.

## Shared: fire, and what it costs here

The canopy burns and a player will think of it. **Let it work, and make it cost something different than it does in a lumber camp.**

- **Any open flame sets a web square alight.** It burns out at the end of the next round; anything in a burning square takes **1d6 fire**, no save; the fire spreads to every contiguous web square at the start of each round.
- **A burning canopy comes down.** At the end of the round a burning section collapses onto the road below: everything under it makes a **[ASK PLAYER] DC 12 Dex save** or takes 1d6 bludgeoning, goes prone, and is on fire. The spiders included. This is a legitimate and very funny way to win.
- **The cost is the wood.** This is standing forest in high summer on the only road to the ruins. If the party burns the canopy and walks away, **roll a d6 at the end of the encounter: on a 1–2 the deadfall catches**, and by evening there is smoke visible from Ardenford's Middle Tier and somebody is going to work out who was on that road. Nobody fines them. It just gets known.
- **In v2, fire kills the pony**, immediately and without a roll, because the pony is hanging *in the web*. Say the pony is hanging in the web out loud, once, before anyone lights anything. Once is fair.

## v1 — The Shortcut

**1 giant spider + 2 giant wolf spiders — 300 XP.** The baseline, and the correct default when the party is already late, already low, or already talking about the goblins.

Nobody has died here yet that the party can see. There is no nest, no cocoon, no story — just a hunting web across a road and three things that were waiting for something the size of a person to walk under it.

**How it opens.** The birdsong stops a quarter mile back — give them that, free, and see who slows down. Let them get thirty feet into the hollow. Then the wolf spiders come out of the leaf litter **behind** the marching order, and the giant spider Webs the front rank from directly overhead.

**How it plays.** Web, drop, bite, climb. Flank, bite, run. It is a grind by design, and the lesson the party takes down the road is *poison is a resource tax and we have no answer to things above us.*

**What it teaches for the road ahead.** Everything here commits and nothing here talks. Three miles further on, the goblins do the exact opposite — they kite, they lie, and they surrender. Landing that contrast is most of what v1 is for.

## v2 — The Cart

**1 giant spider + 2 giant wolf spiders — 300 XP.** Identical roster; the pony and the pedlar have no combat value. Use this when the road should mean something — and when you want the lumber camp job to have a thread running out of it.

**What they see first, before initiative:** a handcart on its side in the ditch at the near lip of the hollow, one wheel off, tin pots scattered and rained on for four days. The tailboard is painted **C. HALLIWELL — TIN & POTS, ARDENFORD**. Twenty feet further on, eight feet up, something horse-shaped is hanging in a sling of web, and it is breathing.

### Sixpence

A stocky brown cart-pony, wrapped from hock to withers, hanging in a purpose-built cradle of web over the road. Poison-drowsy, not dead. There is a brass disc on the collar with **SIXPENCE** stamped into it.

- **This is the larder, and the spiders know exactly where it is.** Cutting at it triggers the **larder rule** — whoever does it owns the giant spider's undivided attention for the rest of the fight.
- **Cutting it down.** The sling is an object: **AC 10, HP 8**, or one action with a blade and no roll if nobody is contesting. Two anchor lines at 10 ft can be cut instead (**AC 10, 3 HP each**) — cut both and the cradle lowers rather than drops.
- **Cut the sling and it falls and it bolts.** 1d6 bludgeoning to the pony, then it comes out of the web at a dead panicked gallop straight down the road: every creature in a 40-ft line makes a **[ASK PLAYER] DC 12 Dex save** or takes **1d6 bludgeoning and is knocked prone**. *Spiders included.* A party that works this out has turned the encounter's hostage into its best weapon, and they should absolutely be allowed to.
- **The soft clock — two lines, said flatly.**
  > *End of round 4:* "The pony has stopped kicking."
  > *End of round 6:* "It's very quiet up there."

  It dies at the end of round 6 if nobody has cut it down. Most versions of this fight are over before then; that is deliberate. This is a clock the party will *notice* and rarely lose to.
- **Sixpence alive is worth more than the loot.** A level-1 party with no money and a pack animal is a different party. Let them keep it if they want it.

### Cobb Halliwell

Wrapped in the canopy at 18 ft, above the cart, dead four days. No stat block, no name to play — he is an object with a story on him. **DC 12 Perception** from the road spots the shape; getting him down is a climb and a cut and the larder rule.

On him: a purse (**6 gp, 14 sp**), a lead-covered tally-book listing four months of stops between Ardenford and the western farms, and a plain ring. In the cart: tin pots, needles, a bolt of oilcloth, ribbon, a whetstone — **about 22 gp of goods** in Ardenford, and far too heavy to carry without the cart. **The cart's axle is sound**; ten minutes and a **DC 12** check with tools re-wheels it, and then the party has a cart and something to pull it.

**Hook, not a trap:** Halliwell's sister keeps a stall in Western Market. Nobody will ever catch a party that keeps the lot — but she is a fixture, she asks after him for months, and a party that brings her the tally-book and the pony gets a permanent friendly face in the market and 15 gp she cannot really afford.

### The tracks — where these three came from

This is the part worth running. **[ASK PLAYER] DC 12 Survival**, or free to anyone who says "where did they come from?":

- **Three sets of tracks came into this hollow together, from the east-south-east, four nights ago.** In a line, not spread out. Not hunting — *travelling*.
- **[ASK PLAYER] DC 13 Nature:** the web here is days old — bright, unsoiled, no husks, no egg sacs, no larder but a pony. **This is not a lair. It is a camp.** A giant spider with a nest does not build a road-web. This one is looking for somewhere to put one.
- **East-south-east through the old wood, three miles, is Gareth Thorne's [[world/factions/ardenhaven/locations/old-lumber-camp/_overview|lumber camp]].**

Play it whichever way the campaign already went:

- **If the party has not done the lumber camp yet:** this is the writ hook arriving early and sideways, and it is much better than reading a notice off a board. There is a nest out there big enough to be pushing animals out of it.
- **If the party has already cleared the lumber camp:** these three left before the party got there — slipped out the south spill when the fight started, or were driven off by a nest that had got too crowded weeks earlier. Either way: *they did not finish it*, and they get to find that out on a road, in the rain, four days late, standing next to a dead pedlar. Do not editorialise. Let them do the arithmetic.
- **Either way, egg sacs.** There are none here yet. There would have been by autumn.

## v3 — Tam Ackley

**1 giant spider + 2 giant wolf spiders — 300 XP.** Tam has no combat value and adds nothing to the budget. Layers cleanly over v1 *or* v2. Use this when the speed bump should hand the party something for the road ahead.

### Tam Ackley

**Sixteen, all elbows, footpost runner between Ardenford and the western farms.** He carries a satchel with a brass Ardenhaven tag on the strap, a walking stick, and nothing that could be called a weapon; running is the entire job and he is very good at it. Forty minutes ago something on the rubble at the Western Scrubland Ruins put an arrow through his satchel at two hundred yards, and he has been sprinting ever since, and he has not looked up once.

**If you need numbers: AC 12, HP 8, no attack you should ever roll.** One bite from the giant spider takes him to 0. At 0 he is unconscious and dying, not dead — the poison gets him first.

### Two lines to steal verbatim

> *(arriving, at a flat sprint, before he has seen anything above head height)* "Don't— there's— there's *things* in the rocks back there, they shot at me, they shot at the *post*—"

> *(afterwards, if he is on his own feet)* "I'm not going back up that road. You can have the letters, I don't care, I'll— " *(stops)* "Tell them Ackley's boy brought them out, though. Will you tell them that."

### How to run him

**He arrives at the top of round 2**, from the far end of the hollow — the direction the party is going. He covers 40 ft into the middle of the fight before anyone can shout, because he is running from the thing behind him and not looking at the thing above him.

- **He is a magnet.** Both wolf spiders' standing instruction is *run down stragglers*, and there is now a straggler sprinting through the middle of the road. **One wolf spider peels off him immediately.** That is one wolf spider not biting a PC, and one boy about to die.
- **Saving him is cheap and should be.** Any PC who spends movement and shouts gets him into the ditch or behind a trunk — **no roll, say yes.** Grabbing him bodily is one action. The cost is the action, not a check, and that is the whole design: he is a round of somebody's turn, not a skill challenge.
- **He does not help.** He will not hold a torch, will not carry anything, will not stop apologising. If left alone he curls up behind the nearest trunk with his arms over his head.

### Why he is worth the round — the payoff

**If Tam walks out of the hollow, the party arrives at the ruins knowing things they would otherwise have to roll for:**

- There is **a lookout up on the rubble** and it watches the road — he was two hundred yards out and still got shot at.
- **He counted five.** Four with bows, and a bigger one, further back, shouting at the others.
- They came *out* of the ruins at him rather than staying under cover, which means they are confident, which means they have been there a while.

That converts the goblin encounter's opening from *"a lookout spots you unless you beat its passive Perception"* into *"you know about the lookout"* — which is a real, earned tactical gift and costs nothing but one round of somebody's turn on a forest road.

**If Tam dies:** the satchel is still there. The letters get delivered by the party or they don't, the postmaster pays **5 gp** for the brass tag and the bag and asks one question the party will not enjoy answering, and nobody at the ruins knows anyone is coming. If he lives, the postmaster pays **15 gp** and a standing favour — free message-running anywhere in Ardenhaven, which for a crew with no name and no sponsor is worth considerably more than fifteen gold.

### Variation — already in the tree

If you'd rather he is *in* trouble than *arriving* in it: Tam has been up a beech at the hollow's far lip **since dawn**, twenty feet off the ground, hoarse, with a wolf spider curled at the base of the trunk that has not moved in six hours. The party's approach is what finally sets the whole thing off. Same boy, same lines, same payoff — but now the fight opens with a hostage already in place and a wolf spider that starts the encounter 60 ft from the party and unbothered by them.

## Loot and aftermath (all variants)

There is nothing on the spiders. **v1 has no loot at all** and should not apologise for it — the reward is arriving at the ruins with most of your hit points.

- **v2:** 6 gp 14 sp on Halliwell, a plain ring, ~22 gp of tin goods, a repairable handcart, a tally-book, and a pony called Sixpence.
- **v3:** 5 gp or 15 gp from the Ardenford postmaster, depending, plus a standing favour that keeps paying.

**Whatever happens, the party is now a mile from the ruins with their poison-drained hit points showing.** The goblin cave is the next thing on this road, and Nimble Escape does not care how tired anybody is. Consider offering a short rest and then charging them the daylight for it.

## NPCs

- **giant-spider** ×1 — the canopy weaver (CR 1, 200 XP). Same in all three variants.
- **giant-wolf-spider** ×2 — ground outriders (CR 1/4 each, 50 XP each). Same in all three variants. Set to 3 in the picker (**350 XP**) for a table of six.

Non-combatants (no DB entries, no picker rows, do not roll initiative for them): **Sixpence** the pony and **Cobb Halliwell** (v2), **Tam Ackley** (v3).

**XP: 200 + 2 × 50 = 300 XP in every variant.**
