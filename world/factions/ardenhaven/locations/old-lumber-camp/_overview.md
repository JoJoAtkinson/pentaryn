# The Old Lumber Camp — Spiders

> Three miles south, forest road past Miller's Creek. The cart track comes out of the trees and the wall is right there — a ring of stacked logs twice a man's height, gate hanging open, nobody on it. Nothing has been cut here in three weeks. The chimney is still smoking.

**Where:** Gareth Thorne's abandoned lumber camp in the southern forest, ~3 miles from Ardenford. Quest: [Spider Lair](../../quests/writ-board-cr1/spider-lair-cr1.md), posted by forester **Gareth Thorne** — 80gp, *"Lost two workers already. Need the camp cleared. That's it."*
**Map:** Roll20 page **"The Old Lumber Camp — Spiders"**, 50×50 squares at 5 ft (250 ft × 250 ft). Palisade ring, stone bunkhouse dead centre, sawpits, log piles, timber stacks, cart road in from the treeline.
**Tuned for:** 4–6 level-1 PCs. **1 giant spider + 2 giant wolf spiders = 300 XP** — Moderate for four, Low for six.

---

## Read this first — how the variants work

There are three ways to run this map. **All three use the identical monster roster and the identical XP.** The picker counts never change: `giant-spider ×1`, `giant-wolf-spider ×2`. Pick a variant on the way to the table — or after the party has already walked in the gate — and nothing needs re-tuning.

| | Variant | Roster | XP | What's actually different |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **v1** | The Clearance | 1 GS + 2 GWS | **300** | Nothing. A clean fight on good terrain. |
| **v2** | The Living | 1 GS + 2 GWS | **300** | Six cocoons, one of them breathing, and a six-round clock. Difficulty comes from divided attention, not more hit points. |
| **v3** | A Name To Play | 1 GS + 2 GWS | **300** | There is a man in the bunkhouse and he does not want you to kill the spiders. |

**Scaling for six:** add a third giant wolf spider (`count: 3`) → **350 XP**, still Low-to-Moderate. Do not add a second giant spider at level 1 — two Webs in the same round on a four-person party is how you get a TPK by accident.

**v3 stacks on v2.** They are written to run together and they are better that way — see *Stacking v2 + v3* at the bottom. XP is unchanged; the survivor has no combat value.

## The camp — terrain that matters

The palisade is the whole design. This is not a roadside skirmish; it is an **arena with two doors**, and the spiders own the walls.

- **The palisade ring.** Stacked, unpeeled logs, ~10 ft high, ringing the camp. **Climbing it is DC 10 Athletics** (5 ft of movement on a success) — but the crown is webbed, and a PC who tops the wall arrives in difficult terrain with a spider already there. All three spiders run the wall top at full speed. *Effect at the table: the party cannot casually leave, and there is no "back up into the trees and shoot it" answer.*
- **Two entrances.**
  - **The cart gate** (north, where the road comes out of the treeline) — 15 ft wide, one leaf hanging off its hinge. The obvious way in and the only easy way out. The wolf spiders know that.
  - **The spill** (south-west) — a collapsed run of palisade where the stacking gave way. Passable, but the loose logs are **difficult terrain** for 15 ft in either direction. A creature knocked prone here takes 1d4 bludgeoning.
- **The bunkhouse.** Stone walls, shake roof, one door, two shuttered windows (one shutter broken inward), a chimney that is **still smoking**. Interior roughly 20 × 30 ft, ceiling 12 ft to the rafters, dark. **This is the nest** — webbing floor to rafters, cocoons hung above head height, egg sacs wedged into the joists. The spiders like it because the flue is warm. Fighting inside neuters the giant spider's climb game but packs the party into one Web. The door is a **choke point** worth using; so is the fact that spiders come down the chimney.
- **Two sawpits.** 6 ft deep, 5 ft × 20 ft, half-covered by rotted plank walkways and a skin of web. Stepping on a covered pit square: **DC 12 Dex or fall in, prone**, 1d6 bludgeoning. Climbing out costs 10 ft of movement. A wolf spider is hiding in one of them at the start. There is an egg sac in the other.
- **Log piles and timber stacks.** Six or seven stacks, 5–8 ft high, scattered through the yard. **Half cover**; climbable for 10 ft of movement; they break line of sight, which is how the wolf spiders get behind people. **They are also a weapon:** a PC can shove a stack with a **DC 13 Athletics** check — logs roll into the adjacent 10 ft, **2d6 bludgeoning, DC 12 Dex for half**, and that ground becomes difficult terrain permanently. Tell a player this is possible the first time one of them looks at a stack.
- **Webbing.** Grey ropes and sheets strung stack-to-stack, palisade-to-eaves, 8–15 ft up, with heavy curtains hanging across the gaps between stacks. Web squares are **difficult terrain**; pushing through a hanging curtain costs a **DC 12 Str check** or the movement is wasted. All three spiders ignore all of it.
- **The drag marks.** In the mud from the gate to the bunkhouse door: two long parallel furrows, boot-heel width, weeks old and rained on. A third set is fresher. Lead with this — it is the whole encounter in one image.
- **Light and weather.** Overcast forest afternoon, dim under the web canopy in the yard's western half, properly **dark inside the bunkhouse**. Cold. Nothing is singing.

### The smoke

**The chimney is smoking and the players will notice immediately.** Each variant answers it differently — decide before they roll Perception:

- **v1:** the sawdust heap beside the near sawpit has been smouldering since the crew left. Sawdust does that for a month. There is nobody alive here.
- **v2:** same smouldering heap — but the cocoon nearest the flue is warm, and the party will not know why that matters until round two.
- **v3:** it is a fire. Someone lit it this morning.

## Complications — put these in front of the players

### The egg sacs (the grey choice)

**Four** of them: three wedged up in the bunkhouse joists, one down in the far sawpit. Each is the size of a curled-up dog and holds a few hundred young, about two weeks from hatching.

- Destroying one is trivially easy — an axe, a torch, a boot. There is no check.
- **Nobody is paying for it.** Gareth's writ says *clear the camp and retrieve the tools*. It does not mention eggs. He will not ask, and he will not pay extra.
- **But the giant spider's larder rule fires:** the moment a sac is damaged, the giant spider drops whatever it is doing and comes for whoever did it, for the rest of the fight, ignoring everyone else. Doing it mid-combat is a deliberate, expensive play. Doing it after the fight is free, and quiet, and a very different scene.
- **If they leave them:** the camp is a lair again inside a month, and it will be a worse one. That's a follow-up writ, and a party who chose it will know they chose it.
- **If they burn them in the bunkhouse:** see below. That is the only stone building in the camp and it is full of web.

### The webbing is flammable — and so is everything Gareth is paying for

Fire is a legitimate, clever, very tempting answer to this map. It should work. It should also **cost**.

- **Igniting web:** any open flame sets a web square alight. It burns out at the end of the next round. Anything in a burning square takes **1d6 fire** (no save); the fire spreads to every contiguous web square at the start of each round.
- **The camp catches.** If burning web is adjacent to a timber stack or a log pile at the start of a round, roll — on a **d6 ≥ 4**, that stack catches, and a burning stack cannot be put out with what the party is carrying.
- **What it costs, concretely — say this to the players *before* they light it if any of them has ever worked wood:** *"That's seasoned oak. That's four months of somebody's summer."*
  - **Each timber stack burned: −10 gp** off Gareth's 80.
  - **The bunkhouse gutted (roof gone, tools inside destroyed): the job is a failure.** No 80gp. Gareth pays nothing, says almost nothing, and that is worse.
  - Burn the whole camp and the forest with it and the party has a considerably larger problem than spiders.
- **In v2, fire in the bunkhouse kills the person they came to save, immediately and without a roll.** This is the sharpest moment on the map: the clever solution and the rescue are the same building. Do not warn them twice. Once is fair.

### The tools (the actual contract)

Gareth wants his tools back and he was specific about it. **Quality woodworking tools, worth 20 gp to him**, cached under the floorboards beneath the third bunk — they are not lying in the open, and a party that clears the camp and leaves without them has completed half a job. **DC 13 Investigation** in the bunkhouse, or ask anyone who worked here. Also in that corner: **45 sp** in a tin, and a **silver locket (12 gp)** that belonged to one of the dead.

## v1 — The Clearance

**1 giant spider + 2 giant wolf spiders — 300 XP. Moderate for four, Low for six.**

The baseline. No one is alive in this camp, no one is going to talk, and the job is exactly what the writ said it was. Run this when the table wants to *fight*, when it is late, or when it is somebody's first session and the lesson should be "terrain is a weapon."

**How it opens.** The party comes up the cart road and through the gate into a silent yard. Let them get thirty or forty feet in — past the first timber stacks, in sight of the bunkhouse door and the drag marks. Then the wolf spiders come out of the sawpit and the log gaps **behind** them, and the giant spider stands up off the chimney.

**How it plays.** Round 1 is a Web from the roof at whoever is nearest the bunkhouse door. The wolf spiders flank, bite, and climb out of reach up the palisade. The big one crosses on the web lines and drops on whatever is Restrained. Nothing here retreats and nothing here can be reasoned with.

**What it teaches.** The stacks are half cover, the stacks can be *pushed over*, the sawpits are a hazard for both sides, and the wall means running is not free. A party that uses the yard wins comfortably; a party that stands in the open in a bunch and swings gets Webbed and ground down by poison.

**The egg sacs and the fire are still on the table.** Even with nobody to rescue, this variant has two real decisions in it. Don't skip them because it's "the simple one."

## v2 — The Living

**1 giant spider + 2 giant wolf spiders — 300 XP. Same roster, same monsters, meaningfully harder in play.**

The extra difficulty is **the actions the party spends not fighting**, and it is priced in rounds, not hit points. The fight is in the way of the encounter; it is not the encounter.

### The cocoons

**Six** of them hang from the bunkhouse rafters and the eaves outside the door. From more than 5 ft away they are identical grey bundles. From the doorway you can see that some of them are person-shaped.

| # | Contents | What a close look shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ilsa Byrne — alive.** Unconscious, poisoned, dying. | Condensation on the inside of the silk. A shallow rise and fall. |
| 2 | **Denny Ostell — dead.** Wrapped three weeks, desiccated. | Lighter than it should be. The **silver locket** is on him. |
| 3 | Roe deer, mostly eaten | Wrong shape once you're near it. Smells. |
| 4 | Empty husk — an old kill, taken out and finished | Slack, collapsed, opens with no resistance. |
| 5 | Empty husk | As above. |
| 6 | **Not a cocoon — an egg sac** wrapped like one | Too round. Moves when the light hits it. Cutting it open is a very bad round. |

**Finding the living one.** From within 5 ft: **DC 13 Perception or Survival** (or Medicine — reward whoever asks). From across the room or the yard: **DC 15**, and even a success only narrows it to two. Each look is an action.

**Cutting one down.** One action, or treat the cocoon as an object — **AC 12, 10 HP**. Then **DC 12 Medicine, or any healing at all**, to stop the venom. She does not wake up; she stops dying.

**The larder rule fires on every one of these.** Cut a cocoon, and the giant spider comes for you and only you. Two PCs checking cocoons is two PCs not fighting, and one of them now has a CR 1 monster with Advantage on its bite committed to them personally. That is where the 300 XP starts hurting.

### The clock — say it out loud

Ilsa is failing. **At the end of round 2, round 4, and round 6, she fails a death save.** No roll, no save, no mercy — the DM just announces it, in the same flat voice, every time:

> *End of round 2:* "Somewhere in the rafters, something stops making noise."
> *End of round 4:* "That's twice."
> *End of round 6:* "It's quiet up there now."

**She dies at the end of round 6** unless she is cut down and stabilised first. Level-1 fights on this map run four to six rounds. That is the point — the party *cannot* do both comfortably, and they have to choose which round they stop swinging.

**Two hard interactions, both worth it:**

- **Fire in the bunkhouse kills her outright.** No roll. If the party's clever answer is a torch through the door, they get a clean, fast, cheap victory and a body. Let them have it and let them find out afterwards.
- **A stabilised Ilsa is not a rescued Ilsa.** She is unconscious and needs carrying — three miles, through the forest, in the dark, on the road where the spiders came from.

### Payoff

Per the quest: Gareth pays **80gp**, plus **20gp and a standing favour** (lumber and supplies at cost, forever) if Ilsa comes back alive. If she doesn't, he pays the 80 anyway, and word gets round the writ board that the party was **slow**. He will not say it to their faces. Someone else will.

## v3 — A Name To Play

**1 giant spider + 2 giant wolf spiders — 300 XP. The survivor has no combat value and adds nothing to the budget.**

The chimney is smoking because someone lit the fire this morning.

### Wick Halloran, sawyer

**Human, mid-forties. Three weeks unwashed. One boot. Hands wrapped in sacking against the cold. Not okay.**

Not a combat-runner NPC — no stat block worth the name, and deliberately so. If you need numbers: **AC 10, HP 6**, no attack you should ever roll. He owns a felling axe and he will not use it on anything that is alive. If the party attacks him he goes into a sawpit and stays there.

**The truth, which he will not lead with.** He was on the crew that ran, three weeks ago, and he did not run. He barred himself in the bunkhouse loft. He is alive — the only warm thing in this camp the spiders have left alone — because after the second day he started **leaving them things**. The salt pork. The camp's mule. And then Denny Ostell, who had a leg broken in three places and was screaming and was not going to see the week out. Wick did not carry Denny anywhere. He just stopped closing the door.

He has spent three weeks building a story he can live inside. The story is called **the arrangement**, and he believes it the way people believe things at four in the morning.

**His fixed idea:** *the arrangement holds*. He is alive because of a bargain. Killing them breaks it. If the party kills them and leaves, the forest is still out there, and the next thing that comes to a camp with no wall-watch and no crew will not have made a deal with him.

**What he wants:** to be told he did the right thing. By strangers. Before he helps with anything.

**How to play him:** half-sentences. He stops mid-word to listen for skittering, and then picks up where he left off as if nothing happened. He answers questions about two beats late, because he has to remember how conversations go. He does not blink much. He is very, very polite.

### Three lines to steal verbatim

> *(through the barred door, before they've seen his face)* "Don't— don't cut those. Whatever else you do. Not the ones up near the roof. **They count them.**"

> "Three weeks. Three weeks, and I'm the only thing in this camp still breathing that they've walked past. You think that's *luck?* That's an arrangement, that is. You come in swinging and you break it — and it's me that pays it. Not you. You'll be gone by dark."

> *(on Denny, if pressed, and only if pressed)* "He was going to die. His leg was — you didn't see it. I never *gave* him to them." *(long pause)* "I just stopped closing the door."

And, if anyone asks what he wants out of this:

> "Tell me I did right. That's all. Just say it out loud. **You weren't here.**"

### What he does — cruel, kind, transactional

**If the party is cruel** — threatens him, calls him a murderer, ties him up, or butchers the egg sacs in front of him while he begs — he stops. Completely. Goes flat and pleasant and gives them nothing: not the tool cache, not which cocoon is breathing, not the chimney. And when he walks back to Ardenford he tells Gareth a version where the party burned his camp and went through his dead men's pockets. Gareth pays — Gareth is fair — but he never uses them again, and the writ board is a small place. *(This is the quest's "word spreads that the party was too slow," with a face on it.)*

**If the party is kind** — lets him talk, doesn't press about Denny, and somebody actually says the words — he comes apart, and then he gives them everything he has: the tools under the third bunk's floorboards, the fact that **the spiders come down the chimney and always have**, that the sacs are in the joists and one is in the far sawpit, and — if you are running v2 — **which cocoon is still breathing**, by name. He walks back to Ardenford with them and tells Gareth about Denny himself, badly, in the street, in front of people.

**If the party is transactional** — pays him, trades him, or offers "help us and we never mention Denny" — he takes it instantly and is genuinely useful: he knows the yard, the pits, the stack that's ready to go over. And he holds them to it forever. He will be in Ardenford in a month, at the wrong moment, in front of the wrong person, checking with his eyes that they're still holding the line. That is a hook, not a loose end. Use it.

### Where he complicates the job

- He will **physically stand in front of the egg sacs.** Not heroically. Badly, with his hands up, apologising.
- He does not want the spiders dead, and the party's contract says the spiders die. There is no version of this where everybody gets what they want.
- **Whatever happens, he is going to tell Gareth something.** The party does not control which version. They only influence it.
- He is a witness to what the party does to a nest full of eggs when they think nobody is watching.

### Optional twist — the lie in the writ

If you want the writ board itself to have been dishonest: **Wick is Gareth's brother-in-law.** Gareth's sister's husband. That is why the notice is on expensive paper with no emotion on it, why it says *no questions*, and why Gareth "rarely makes eye contact" — he has known for three weeks that there might be somebody in there, and he priced it at 80 gold and posted it on a board rather than walk down that road himself. Everything above still runs unchanged. It just means the party's report to Gareth lands on a man who already knew.

### Stacking v2 + v3

Run them together. It costs nothing and it is the best version of this map.

**Wick knows which cocoon is breathing, and he will not tell them.** Not out of malice — out of arithmetic. If the spiders lose Ilsa, they will want something else, and there is only one other warm thing in this camp. He knows exactly what he is doing and he cannot make himself say it. The clock runs at the end of rounds 2, 4 and 6 while a man stands in the doorway not saying a name.

**Kindness stops the clock.** If a PC reaches him — actually reaches him, not a Persuasion roll but a moment — he says "*third one along, by the flue, she was still going this morning*" and the party gets to skip straight to the cutting. That is the whole encounter paying off in one line, and it is worth building the session around.

## NPCs

- **giant-spider** ×1 — the nest weaver, on the bunkhouse roof by the warm flue (CR 1, 200 XP)
- **giant-wolf-spider** ×2 — the yard hunters, in the sawpits and the log gaps (CR 1/4, 50 XP each)

**Total: 300 XP in every variant.** Set `giant-wolf-spider` to 3 in the picker (350 XP) for a table of six.

Non-combatants (no DB entries, no picker rows, do not roll initiative for them): **Wick Halloran** (v3), **Ilsa Byrne** (v2, unconscious throughout).
