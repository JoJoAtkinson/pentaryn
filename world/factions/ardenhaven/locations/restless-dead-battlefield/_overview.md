# Restless Dead — Eastern Valley Battlefield

> Four of them, strung out in a loose column, walking west. Not hunting. Not hunting anything. Just walking, the way soldiers walk when they've been told to go home.

**Where:** a windswept valley in the eastern mountains, ~4 miles from Ardenford — an abandoned battlefield from a war that ended 127 years ago. Quest: [Restless Dead](../../quests/writ-board-cr1/restless-dead-cr1.md), posted by border-patrol scout **Renn Caldor**.
**Tuned for:** 4–6 level-1 PCs. **200 XP in every variant** at the four-PC roster — pick one on the way to the table and nothing needs re-tuning. The XP is deliberately soft; the encounter's weight is emotional, not mechanical.

---

## Read this first — how the variants work

There are three ways to run this valley. **All three sit on the same 200 XP** for four PCs, and all three scale the same way for five or six (**+50 XP**, one more body). The terrain, the cairns, Corwin, the pauldron, and the "Maris" ring are **shared and unchanged across all of them**. Only the *reason the dead are walking* changes.

| | Variant | Roster (4 PCs) | XP | What's actually different |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **v1** | [The Long Walk Home](#v1--the-long-walk-home) | skeleton ×4 | **200** | Nothing. Four confused soldiers going home. The default, and the emotional core. |
| **v2** | [The One That Doesn't Stop](#v2--the-one-that-doesnt-stop) | skeleton ×3 + zombie ×1 | **200** | One of them can't be talked to. The party's clever answer half-works. |
| **v3** | [Ambrose Wend and his procession](#v3--ambrose-wend-and-his-procession) | skeleton ×3 + ambrose-wend ×1 | **200** | There is a chaplain walking them home, he thinks it's Saturday, and he will not stop calling them back up. |

**Scaling for five or six:** add one more skeleton (`skeleton` count **4**) → **250 XP** in v2 and v3, **250 XP** in v1 with a fifth. Still Low-to-Moderate at level 1.

**Picker counts, so nobody has to think at the table:**

| Variant | `skeleton` | `zombie` | `ambrose-wend` |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| v2 | 3 (or 4) | 1 | 0 |
| v3 | 3 (or 4) | 0 | 1 |
| v2 + v3 stacked | 3 | 1 | 1 (**250 XP**) |

**v2 and v3 stack cleanly** — see *Stacking v2 + v3* at the bottom of v3. It is the meanest version of this valley and it is very good.

## Shared: terrain

- **Valley floor:** rocky, open, no cover to speak of. Loose stone in patches — difficult terrain, and a Dash across it is a bad idea.
- **Burial cairns:** waist-to-chest-high heaps of stacked stone scattered across the field, plus weathered grave markers. Half cover. Climbable in 5 ft of movement.
- **Elevation:** the valley shoulders rise on both sides in rough steps. A PC on a cairn or a ledge is out of shortsword reach — skeletons will switch to the shortbow rather than climb.
- **Loose stone:** there are rocks everywhere, and rocks are **bludgeoning**. Skeletons are Vulnerable to bludgeoning — a thrown stone, a cairn shoved over, a quarterstaff, a mace: all double damage. Reward players who notice.
- **Light and weather:** cold, bright, constant wind. Full daylight, long sightlines — the party sees the column coming from a long way off, which is the whole point. They have time to decide.

## Shared: Corwin

One of the skeletons wears a **corroded bronze pauldron with a faint oak-tree emblem** — the mark of the Ridgeward Battalion. That is **Corwin Caldor**, Renn's grandfather. Statistically identical to the others; the DM should just decide which one it is before initiative and keep it consistent. Among his bones: Ardenhaven military insignia, rotted personal effects, **18 sp**, and one intact silver ring engraved **"Maris"**.

If the party lays the remains in a cairn with proper rites (**DC 10 Religion**), Renn is undone by it — see the quest file for the payout and the follow-on hooks.

**Corwin is in every variant, unchanged.** He is always a skeleton, always wears the pauldron, always carries the ring. The zombie in v2 is not him and has nothing to do with him. The chaplain in v3 knew him personally and can say his wife's name — which is either the best moment in the encounter or the worst, depending where you sit.

## v1 — The Long Walk Home

**skeleton ×4 — 200 XP. A fair fight for four, easy for six. Add a fifth in the count spinner for a bigger table.**

The default, and the reason this location exists. Run it as written.

### How it opens

**It doesn't open as a fight.** The skeletons are already walking when the party arrives, heading west toward Ardenford, and they will keep walking past the party if allowed to. They do not attack unless attacked, blocked, or grabbed. The party gets a full round — or a full ten minutes — to decide what this is.

Three ways it goes:

1. **Swords out.** Someone attacks. That skeleton fights back. Its neighbours only join in as they're individually hit or blocked, so a party that starts a fight can still stop one.
2. **Words first.** A PC speaks gently instead of swinging — **DC 13 Persuasion or Religion** reveals the skeletons aren't hostile at all: they're confused, and they're going *home*. They stop and listen while a calm voice keeps talking. This is `heed_the_living`, and it is the pivot the whole encounter turns on.
3. **Let them pass.** A legitimate option, and a bad one — they reach Ardenford in a day and a half and the party owns the consequence.

### Tactics

Skeletons fight defensively and without strategy: nearest target, no flanking, no focus fire, no morale, no pursuit. If the party disengages, the skeletons reorient west and resume walking. They do not chase. Anything on elevation or behind a gap gets shot at rather than chased. There is no leader and nothing to parley with — but there is something to *talk to*.

### Payoff

The good ending here is four sets of bones in four cairns and a silver ring in Renn Caldor's hand. **A party that never rolls initiative gets full XP.** Say so.

## v2 — The One That Doesn't Stop

> The default column above is unchanged. This variant adds one figure at the back of it.

There is a fifth figure trailing the column, and it is not keeping the column's pace. A **zombie** — a *Calderon* soldier, wrong uniform, wrong side of the field, and no cairn over him a hundred and twenty-seven years ago. Where the others are walking *home*, this one is only walking.

**Two rosters, both honest about the maths:**

| Setup | XP | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **3 skeletons + 1 zombie** (swap one out) | **200 XP** — identical to the v1 budget | Same encounter weight on paper, meaningfully harder in play. Undead Fortitude gives the zombie a second life the XP table doesn't price. Use this for four PCs. |
| **4 skeletons + 1 zombie** (add him on) | **250 XP** | The right call for five or six. |

**Why he's worth adding:**

- **Undead Fortitude is the whole point.** 0 HP triggers a **Con save, DC 5 + damage taken** (unless the killing damage was **Radiant** or a **Critical Hit**) — on a success he drops to 1 HP and keeps going. Say the DC out loud every time. The party learns, in about two rounds, that chipping something for 4s can go on all night and that one big hit ends it. That is a lesson worth a whole level of play.
- **He breaks the bludgeoning rule the party just learned.** Everything on this field so far has taken **double** from rocks and maces. He doesn't. They will try it, it will do nothing special, and they will need a beat to work out that the answer changed.
- **He cannot be talked to.** This is the real reason he's here. If the party solves the battlefield the good way — a calm voice, **Heed the Living**, DC 13 Persuasion or Religion — they end up with three skeletons standing quiet in a ring and one dead man walking through the middle of them at somebody, with the calm still holding. There is nothing in him to reach. **Deaf To The Living** has no check and no DC; it just doesn't work. The party's clever answer half-works, and they have to finish the job with their hands while three skeletons watch.
- **He's slow.** 20 ft. against a party's 30 ft. Kiting him is legitimate, and so is walking away — same as the skeletons. Don't take that option off the table just because this one is hostile.

**Corwin is unaffected.** He is still one of the skeletons, still wearing the bronze pauldron, still Renn's grandfather. The zombie is not Ardenhaven, is not Ridgeward Battalion, and has nothing to do with the ring or the rites. Keep them separate — the contrast between the man who gets a cairn and the man who never got one is doing the work.

## v3 — Ambrose Wend and his procession

**skeleton ×3 + ambrose-wend ×1 — 200 XP. Identical budget to v1. `skeleton` to 4 → 250 XP for five or six.**

Full stat sheet, voice, and every roll: [`npcs/ambrose-wend.md`](npcs/ambrose-wend.md).

There is a man walking with them. He is walking **backwards, ahead of the column**, so he can keep an eye on them. He is counting heads. There are three heads and he keeps getting eleven.

### Chaplain Ambrose Wend

*Ridgeward Battalion, third company. About fifty. Grey at the temples, ink on the fingers, a censer-headed mace he has never much liked carrying.*

He came up this valley with the battalion a hundred and twenty-seven years ago, and when the ridge fell he was the only officer left standing, so the burying was his. He got about a third of the way through the office for the dead before the Calderon second wave came over the shoulder of the valley and the field was abandoned mid-rite.

**The rite never closed. Neither did he.**

He does not think he is raising anybody. **He thinks he is escorting a funeral procession home** — walking the wounded west to Ardenford, to their families, and he is three days out and behind schedule and very apologetic about it. He has been three days out since the reign before last.

He is not undead. He is not alive either. No pulse, and warm — **DC 12 Medicine**, or just anyone who takes his wrist. Do not explain it. Let a player find it.

### Why he's the right antagonist for this valley

Everything the encounter already does, he makes worse in the good direction. The skeletons walk west because they're going home; he is the *reason* they're going home, and he thinks he's **helping**. He knew Corwin Caldor. He can say **Maris** out loud, unprompted, smiling, because Corwin talked about her constantly and Ambrose promised to get him to the door. He does not contradict one word of the existing quest — he explains it, and the explanation is worse than the mystery was.

**He is not evil and there is no version of this where he is.** The party's job is horrible and it should feel horrible.

### He is genuinely killable — this is not a boss

**HP 11 · AC 13 · CR 1/4 (50 XP) · no resistances, no immunities, no condition immunities.**

Two good hits from a level-1 fighter kill him. He can be poisoned, frightened, tripped, grappled, gagged, and knocked out with the flat of a blade. **The threat on this field is the column. He is only the reason the column is walking.** Do not give him more hit points. Do not give him a phase two.

His offense is one ranged attack (**Radiant Flame**, +4, **2d6 radiant**, 60 ft) that he **will not use at all** until a PC has put one of his men down, plus a mace he is bad at and apologises for.

### The controller-break — say this to the table as a rule

**The skeletons in v3 are not independently animate. They are following him.** No DM discretion, no save, no DC:

**The procession breaks the instant Ambrose is:**
- reduced to **0 HP** (dead *or* knocked out — **nonlethal counts**, and a party that works that out deserves the win), **or**
- **grappled, restrained, incapacitated, stunned, paralysed, or held prone**, **or**
- **unable to speak** — gagged, a hand over his mouth, *Silence*, a successful *Command*, shoved face-down in the scree, **or**
- he **calls the halt himself**.

**Then:** at the **start of the next skeleton's turn**, every remaining skeleton **stops where it stands, mid-swing.** Immediate. Automatic. They stand still for one full round. At the end of that round —

- **He's still down or still stopped** → every skeleton turns west and reverts to **Shamble Onward**. v1 rules resume *exactly*: no attacks unless individually damaged, blocked, or grabbed; no pursuit. **The fight is over** unless the party restarts it.
- **He got free** → he can start them again, but it costs his **entire action**, every time. Grappling him twice is a legitimate and excellent strategy and the fight rewards it.

### Why the fight feels endless until they work it out — `On Your Feet`

**3 uses** (4 on the six-PC roster). When one of his men drops, Ambrose calls him **by name, out loud**, and at the start of that skeleton's next turn it stands back up with **4 HP**.

- **Each individual skeleton can be called back once, ever.** A man who goes down twice stays down.
- **It doesn't work** on a skeleton destroyed by **radiant damage**, by a **critical hit**, or whose bones a PC has **scattered or laid in a cairn** (one action, no check — tell them this is possible the first time one of them looks at a cairn).

**On paper that's 200 XP; in play it's harder, and it has a switch on it.** That's the trade, and it's a fair one, because **he announces it out loud every single time**. He is telling the party how to beat him and he does not know that is what he is doing. A party that stands in the open trading swings loses slowly and never understands why. A party that grabs the chaplain wins in two rounds.

He also has **Bless**, 1/day, on up to three of his men — +1d4 to their attacks and saves for a minute, concentration, and he is a terrible concentrator.

### The fixed delusion — he never breaks from it

**The battle was three days ago. The men are tired, not dead. He is their chaplain and he is getting them to the door.**

Nothing breaks it. Not evidence, not argument, not a natural 20. **There is no Persuasion DC that makes Ambrose Wend understand what he is** — a 24 buys a kind, attentive, completely useless answer, and it should, because the alternative is a man realising he has been carrying corpses for a century, and he cannot survive knowing that. Something in him knows he cannot survive it. That's why it holds.

| The party says | He hears / answers |
|---|---|
| *"They're skeletons."* | "They're **tired**." Flat — the first flat thing he's said. |
| *"It's been 127 years."* | He almost gets there. Counts on his fingers. Loses it. "The road's been longer than I remembered, that's all." |
| *"You're dead too."* | He does not process the sentence. He answers the question you asked **two questions ago**, kindly, and that's worse. |
| *"That one is my friend's grandfather."* | Genuine, awful delight. "Caldor! You're *family?* Oh — that's marvellous. He's just here. Come and tell him." |

**Reasoned with** → engages fully, courteously, at length, concedes nothing; he out-argues them inside his own frame. **Threatened** → doesn't flinch, because it isn't relevant. *"I'm the chaplain. You can do what you like to me after I've got them to the ford."* **Mocked** → he genuinely does not hear it; he replies to the last sincere thing anybody said, and the joke dies in the air. **Pitied** → the only one that gets near him, and he shuts it down fast and pleasantly, then finds something to do with the column and won't look at them for a while.

### Five lines to steal verbatim

> *(Opening. Before anyone has drawn anything. He is **delighted** to see them.)*
> "Ah — thank God. **Thank God.** Are you the relief column? We were told there'd be a relief column at the ford." *(Already looking past them, counting.)* "I've got eleven still walking and I've lost the cart. Have you got water? Not for me. For them."

> *(On being told what they are.)*
> "They're **tired**. Do you understand me? They have been walking for three days and they are *tired*, and you will not stand there and look at them like that where they can hear you."

> *(The first time one of his men goes down. The line the whole variant is built around.)*
> "**Serjeant.** Serjeant, on your feet. We're not there yet." *(Then, to whoever did it, conversationally, the way you'd explain a rule to a child:)* "You don't get to leave him here. His mother is *four miles* that way."

> *(If the skeleton with the bronze pauldron drops. He is smiling.)*
> "Corwin Caldor. Third company, Ridgeward. He has a wife at Ardenford — **Maris**. He talks about her. He talks about her the entire time, he does not *stop*." *(Beat.)* "I promised him I'd get him to the door."

> *(The halt. Quietly first. Then the parade-ground voice, and it cracks.)*
> "...Halt. — **COLUMN. HALT.**"
> *(And they do. Every one of them. Instantly. Because they have been waiting a hundred and twenty-seven years for somebody to tell them they can stop.)*

**Voice notes:** warm, brisk, unhurried, and *busy* — a man in the middle of a job with a list in his head. He interrupts himself to check the column. He apologises for the delay roughly every ninety seconds. He calls the party "gentlemen" regardless of who they are and does not notice doing it. He is never once cruel, never once threatening, and never once wrong about anything except the single fact at the centre of him.

### The other way out — closing the office

**There is a non-violent solve, it is the good ending, and it pays full XP.**

- **DC 13 Religion or Insight** (an action, or free for anyone who just watches him for a round): this is not necromancy. It is a **burial rite**, interrupted, still running. He is the officiant. Nothing here can rest because nobody ever said the last third of the words.
- **DC 15 Religion or History**, one action, spoken aloud over him — **the closing of the office**. Any faith, any tradition; a cleric or paladin of anyone at all, a PC with a holy symbol, or frankly anybody who has been to a funeral and paid attention. These are the words he never got said over *himself*, because by the time it was his turn there was nobody left standing to say them.
- **Success:** the procession ends. Every skeleton lies down where it stands, without drama, the way a man sits down after a long day. Ambrose gets his last line and is not there any more.
- **Failure:** nothing bad happens. He doesn't notice and doesn't retaliate. Try again next round, as often as they like.

> *(His last line. For one second he is not confused at all.)*
> "Oh. — I did lose them, didn't I." *(And then, to the party, and it is the only order he ever gives them:)* "See them the rest of the way."

**Award full 200 XP.** Say it out loud. And note what that last line does: it hands the party the burial job, from the man who started it, and Renn Caldor is waiting at the other end of it.

### What v3 changes about the v1 pivot

**`Heed the Living` still works — and it stops working again.** A skeleton talked down with the DC 13 check stands quiet exactly as it does in v1, until Ambrose's next turn, when `the_column_moves` puts it back in the line. He isn't countering the party; he's calling his man back into formation because the man had stopped, and they're behind schedule.

That is the design: **the answer that solved v1 does not solve v3.** They have to go through him. Same shape as the zombie's *Deaf To The Living* in v2 — the clever solution half-works and the party has to find the other half.

### Loot

A censer-headed **mace** (mundane — the radiant was *him*, not the weapon), a waterlogged **battalion prayer book**, and the **third company muster roll**: 127 years old, still legible, every man in the company listed by name and village. **Corwin Caldor is on it.** Renn would give a great deal for that page, and the veterans' community in Ardenford rather more — that's a follow-on hook with a hundred names in it.

### Stacking v2 + v3

**skeleton ×3 + zombie ×1 + ambrose-wend ×1 = 250 XP.** Run it for five or six, or for four if the table wants teeth.

**Ambrose does not command the zombie.** He tries. He calls it by a name that isn't its name — twice, patiently, then a third time with the parade-ground voice — and it does not stop.

It is the only moment all encounter where **he looks frightened**, and the party will see it, and it is the closest thing to a crack in him that anybody is ever going to get. It doesn't break the delusion. It just means that for about four seconds, a man who has been calmly wrong for a hundred and twenty-seven years does not know what to do.

Then he goes back to counting heads.
