# Zombie

**HP** 15 (2d8+6) **·** **AC** 8 (rotted battalion coat) **·** **Speed** 20 ft. **·** **Con save** +3 **·** **Immune** poison damage; exhaustion, poisoned **·** **Darkvision** 60 ft. **·** **Passive Perception** 8 **·** **CR** 1/4 (50 XP)

> Action mechanics live in `combat-runner/actions.jsonl` — see the launcher's **Ready actions** reference for verbs.

**Languages:** none. Not "understands but cannot speak" — **none**. This is the difference between it and the skeletons walking beside it, and it is the entire point of the variant.

**Undead Fortitude.** If damage reduces it to 0 HP it makes a **Con save (+3), DC 5 + the damage taken**, unless that damage was **Radiant** or came from a **Critical Hit**. On a success it drops to **1 HP** instead and keeps going.

---

## Start-of-turn checklist

1. **Did it hit 0 HP since its last turn?** → **Undead Fortitude**, immediately, before anything else. Not radiant, not a crit → it rolls. **Say the DC out loud** — `5 + damage taken` — before the die comes down. The table needs to watch a 4-damage hit set DC 9 and fail to finish it, and then watch a 12-damage hit set DC 17 and end it. That lesson is worth more than the encounter.
2. **Is it threatened?** Doesn't matter. Unlike the skeletons, it does not need to be provoked. It closes and it attacks.
3. **Is someone talking to it?** → **Deaf To The Living.** No check, no DC, no partial success. Heed the Living does not work on this one.

---

## Tactics — when the DM asks "what does it do?"

- **Default, every round:** close at its full **20 ft** on the nearest living creature and **Slam**. That's it. There is no round-1 opener and no plan.
- **It does not fight defensively like the skeletons do.** The skeletons walk until you make them fight. This one is already fighting. It has been for four days.
- **No target priority, no flanking, no focus fire.** Nearest thing. If two are equidistant, the one it was already facing.
- **It is slow.** 20 ft against a party's 30 ft is a real handle: a party that keeps moving can kite it indefinitely across open valley floor, and a party that has worked out Undead Fortitude might prefer to. Let them. Walking away is a legitimate answer here, exactly as it is with the skeletons.
- **When the party talks the skeletons down:** it keeps coming. Three skeletons stand quiet in a ring while one dead Calderon soldier walks through the middle of them at somebody, and the party has to decide what to do about him with the calm still holding. **This is the variant.** Run it.
- **It is not vulnerable to bludgeoning.** The party will have learned that rocks and maces double against skeletons. They are going to try it on this one, and it is going to do nothing special, and they will need a beat to notice. Radiant damage and big single hits are the answer here, not the same answer as last round.
- **Below 5 HP:** no fear, no retreat, no morale. It keeps swinging. And then it hits 0 and rolls Undead Fortitude and possibly does not stop even then.

## Who it was

A **Calderon** soldier, not an Ardenhaven one — the wrong side of the field, and the wrong uniform to be lying among the Ridgeward Battalion's cairns. Enough of the coat is left to read the cut of it. If [[world/factions/ardenhaven/locations/restless-dead-battlefield/_overview|Corwin]] and the other three are walking home, this one is not: it is only walking.

Nobody laid a cairn over him a hundred and twenty-seven years ago, and that is not an accident. If the party asks why the four are gentle and the fifth is not, the honest DM answer is that nobody knows — but the field knows who it buried and who it didn't.

## Description (one line)

A grey, water-swollen thing in the rotted red of a Calderon field coat, moving at a steady walking pace with its head slightly turned, as if listening to something behind it.
