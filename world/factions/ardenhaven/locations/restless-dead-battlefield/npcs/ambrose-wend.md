# Chaplain Ambrose Wend

**HP** 11 (2d8+2) **·** **AC** 13 (battalion cassock over mail scraps) **·** **Speed** 30 ft. **·** **Saves** Str +2, Wis +2 **·** **Skills** Medicine +4, Religion +2 **·** **No damage resistances, no immunities, no condition immunities** **·** **Passive Perception** 12 **·** **CR** 1/4 (50 XP)

> Action mechanics live in `combat-runner/actions.jsonl` — see the launcher's **Ready actions** reference for verbs.

**He is not undead.** Read that again before you run him. He is a Humanoid with eleven hit points and no immunities to anything. He can be shot, stabbed, poisoned, frightened, charmed, tripped, grappled, gagged, and knocked out with the flat of a blade. **Two good hits from a level-1 fighter kill him.** That is deliberate and it is the entire design: the threat on this field is the column, and he is only the reason the column is walking.

**He is also not alive.** If a PC gets hands on him — **DC 12 Medicine**, or just anyone holding his wrist — there is **no pulse, and he is warm**. He does not eat, does not sleep, has not aged a day in a hundred and twenty-seven years, and does not know any of that. Do not explain it. Let a player find it.

**Languages:** Common. He talks constantly, in complete sentences, politely, and he will answer any question you ask him. None of the answers are true.

---

## Who he is

**Chaplain Ambrose Wend, Ridgeward Battalion, third company.** Fifty-ish. Grey at the temples, ink on the fingers, a censer-headed mace he has never much liked carrying. He came up the valley with the battalion a hundred and twenty-seven years ago, and when the ridge fell he was the only officer left standing, and so the burying was his.

He got about a third of the way through the office for the dead before the Calderon second wave came over the shoulder of the valley and the field was abandoned mid-rite.

**The rite never closed.** Neither did he.

He does not think he is raising anybody. He thinks he is **walking the wounded home**. He has eleven men still on their feet — he is quite firm about the number, and there have not been eleven for a very long time — and he is taking them west to Ardenford, to their families, and he is **three days out and behind schedule** and very apologetic about it.

He has been three days out since the reign before last.

---

## The fixed delusion — he never breaks from it

**The battle was three days ago. The men are tired, not dead. He is their chaplain and he is getting them to the door.**

That is the whole of it, and **nothing breaks it.** Not evidence, not argument, not a natural 20, not the bones in his own hands. There is no Persuasion DC that makes Ambrose Wend understand what he is. If a player rolls a 24 on Persuasion, they get a *kind, attentive, completely useless answer* — and they should, because the alternative is a man realising he has been carrying corpses for over a century, and he cannot survive knowing that. Something in him knows he cannot survive it. That's why the delusion holds.

**How he absorbs contradictions** — he never gets angry, he never argues, he *re-frames*:

| The party says | He hears / answers |
|---|---|
| *"They're skeletons."* | "They're **tired**." Flat, and it's the first flat thing he's said. |
| *"It's been 127 years."* | He almost gets there. Counts on his fingers. Loses it. "The road's been longer than I remembered, that's all. Roads do that." |
| *"You're dead too."* | He does not process the sentence. He answers the question you asked **two questions ago**, kindly, and that's worse. |
| *"That one is my friend's grandfather."* | Delight. Genuine, awful delight. "Caldor! You're *family?* Oh — oh, that's marvellous. He's just here. Come and tell him." |

### Reasoned with · threatened · mocked · pitied

- **Reasoned with:** he engages fully, courteously, at length, and concedes nothing. He is not stupid — he is a literate man who has read more than most of the party — and he will out-argue them inside his own frame. Every fact they present gets absorbed as a detail about the *march*, not about the men.
- **Threatened:** he doesn't flinch, because it isn't relevant. *"I'm the chaplain."* As if that settles it. It has always settled it. *"You can do what you like to me after I've got them to the ford."* He will stand still and let a PC put a blade against him and go on giving marching directions over their shoulder.
- **Mocked:** **he doesn't hear it.** Not "ignores it" — genuinely does not register mockery as a category of speech. He replies to the last sincere thing anybody said. A table that tries to be funny at him gets a chaplain gently asking whether they've eaten today, and the joke dies in the air. Let it.
- **Pitied:** this is the only one that gets near him, and he shuts it down fast, pleasantly. *"You're being very gentle with me. People do that when they've decided something about you."* If a PC keeps at it he goes brisk and busy and finds something to do with the column, and he will not look at them for a while.

## Verbatim — steal these

> *(Opening. Before anyone has drawn anything. He is delighted to see them.)*
> "Ah — thank God. **Thank God.** Are you the relief column? We were told there'd be a relief column at the ford." *(He is already looking past them, counting.)* "I've got eleven still walking and I've lost the cart. Have you got water? Not for me. For them."

> *(On being told what they are.)*
> "They're **tired**. Do you understand me? They have been walking for three days and they are *tired*, and you will not stand there and look at them like that where they can hear you."

> *(On the hundred and twenty-seven years. A long pause. He almost gets there.)*
> "...No. No — the ridge fell on the Thursday, and we came down off it on the Saturday, and this is —" *(He stops. He counts on his fingers. He loses it, and the loss doesn't show.)* "This is the Saturday. The road's been longer than I remembered, that's all. Roads do that."

> *(The first time one of his men goes down. This is the line the whole variant is built around.)*
> "**Serjeant.** Serjeant, on your feet. We're not there yet." *(Then, to whoever did it, conversationally, the way you'd explain a rule to a child:)* "You don't get to leave him here. His mother is *four miles* that way."

> *(If the skeleton with the bronze pauldron drops. He is smiling when he says it.)*
> "Corwin Caldor. Third company, Ridgeward. He has a wife at Ardenford — **Maris**. He talks about her. He talks about her the entire time, he does not *stop*." *(Beat.)* "I promised him I'd get him to the door."

> *(The halt. Very quietly first. Then in the parade-ground voice, and it cracks.)*
> "...Halt. — **COLUMN. HALT.**"

> *(His last line, if the party closes the office over him. For one second he is not confused at all.)*
> "Oh. — I did lose them, didn't I." *(And then, to the party, and it is the only order he ever gives them:)* "See them the rest of the way."

**How to play the voice:** warm, brisk, unhurried, and *busy*. He is a man in the middle of a job with a list in his head. He interrupts himself to check on the column. He apologises for the delay roughly every ninety seconds. He calls the party "gentlemen" regardless of who they are and does not notice doing it. He is never once cruel, never once threatening, and never once wrong about anything except the single fact at the centre of him.

## Breaking the procession — the rule, not a suggestion

**The skeletons in this variant are not independently animate. They are following him.** This is a stated mechanic with no DM discretion in it. Say it to the table the moment they work it out; they've earned it.

**The procession breaks the instant Ambrose is any of:**

- reduced to **0 HP** (dead *or* knocked out — nonlethal counts, and a party that works that out deserves the win),
- **grappled, restrained, incapacitated, stunned, paralysed, or knocked prone and held**,
- **unable to speak** — gagged, a hand over his mouth, *Silence*, a successful *Command*, a shove into the creek, anything,
- or he **calls the halt himself** (see below).

**What happens:** at the **start of the next skeleton's turn**, every remaining skeleton **stops where it stands, mid-swing.** Immediate. Automatic. No save, no DC, no roll. They stand still for one full round.

**Then:**

- **If he is still down or still stopped at the end of that round** — every skeleton turns west and reverts to **Shamble Onward**. The v1 rules resume *exactly*: they do not attack unless individually damaged, blocked, or grabbed, and they do not pursue. The fight is over unless the party restarts it.
- **If he gets free** — breaks the grapple, spits out the gag, stands up — he can start them again, but it costs his **entire action** (`take_up_the_march`, folded into `the_column_moves`). He loses a full turn to it every single time. Grappling him twice is a legitimate and excellent strategy and the fight should reward it.

**This is the lesson the encounter is for.** A level-1 party that stands in the open trading swings with three skeletons while a man in a cassock keeps calling them back up will lose slowly and never understand why. A party that grabs the chaplain wins in two rounds. **Let them find it — but make it findable:** he announces `On Your Feet` out loud, by name, every time he uses it. He is telling them how to beat him. He just doesn't know that's what he's doing.

## The other way out — closing the office

**There is a non-violent solve, it is the good ending, and it pays full XP.**

- **DC 13 Religion or Insight** (an action, or free for anyone who watches him for a round): this is not necromancy. It is a **burial rite**, badly interrupted, still running. He is the officiant. The reason nothing here can rest is that nobody ever said the last third of the words.
- **DC 15 Religion or History**, one action, spoken aloud over him — **the closing of the office**. Any faith. Any tradition. A PC with a holy symbol, a cleric or paladin of anyone at all, or frankly anybody who has been to a funeral and paid attention. It is the words he never got said over *himself*, because by the time it was his turn there was nobody left standing to say them.
- **On a success:** the procession ends. Every skeleton lies down where it stands, without drama, the way a man sits after a long day. Ambrose says his last line and is not there any more — a cassock, a mace, and a muster roll on the stones.
- **On a failure:** nothing bad happens. He doesn't notice, doesn't take offence, doesn't retaliate. Try again next round.
- **Award the full encounter XP.** Say so out loud. A party that ends this without a fight should walk away knowing they were paid for it.

## Start-of-turn checklist

1. **Is the procession broken?** — is he at 0 HP, grappled, restrained, incapacitated, prone-and-held, or unable to speak? If **yes**, he takes no action, and the skeletons run the break rule above. Everything else on this list is skipped.
2. **Has he been freed since last turn?** If he was stopped and is now loose, his **entire action** this turn is restarting the column (`the_column_moves`). No attack, no raise, nothing else.
3. **Did one of his men drop since his last turn?** → `on_your_feet` if he has a use left and that man hasn't already been called back once. **Say the name out loud.** Then check `he_has_a_wife` fired as a reaction when it happened.
4. **`On Your Feet` uses:** starts at **3** (one per skeleton on the field; **4** if running the six-PC roster). Each individual skeleton can be called back **once, ever**. Not usable on a skeleton destroyed by **radiant damage**, by a **critical hit**, or whose bones a PC has **scattered or laid in a cairn** (one action, no check).
5. **`Bless the Column`:** 1/day. Unused? Round 2 is usually the moment.
6. **Has anybody put one of his men down yet?** Until somebody has, he **does not attack**, at all, under any provocation short of being hit himself.

## Tactics — when the DM asks "what does it do?"

- **Round 1, and every round until the party makes it a fight:** `the_column_moves`. He walks west with his men, calls the cadence, apologises for the delay, and tries to give the party jobs. **The encounter opens exactly like v1 opens** — nobody is hostile, nothing is hunting, and the party has all the time in the world to decide what this is.
- **He never opens the fight.** Never. Not if they draw steel, not if they block the road, not if they insult him. The first violence on this field is always the party's.
- **The moment a skeleton drops:** `he_has_a_wife` fires as a reaction — he says the man's name and who is waiting for him — and *then* he becomes a combatant. From his next turn on, the party are **deserters and grave-robbers**, and he is an officer with the authority to discipline them. He is quite calm about it.
- **His priority is always the column, never the kill.** Every turn, ask: is one of my men down and callable? If yes, `on_your_feet`. If no, `radiant_flame` at whoever put the last one down. He does not focus-fire the squishiest PC; he shoots the one who is *interfering with the march*.
- **`Radiant Flame` (+4, 2d6 radiant, 60 ft) is his real weapon** and he stays at range with it, behind his men, ideally on a cairn. At 11 HP he cannot afford a melee round and he is not stupid enough to take one.
- **`Mace` is a last resort** and he is bad at it, and he apologises while swinging. Note the ugly detail: his mace does **radiant** damage, so it is the one thing on this field that can put his own men down for good. He has never once noticed.
- **Below 5 HP:** no retreat, no morale check, no surrender — but not bravado either. He simply keeps working. If he goes down mid-sentence, he goes down mid-sentence.
- **He will not leave the field.** He physically cannot go west faster than the slowest man in the column, and he will not go anywhere else. If the party runs, he does not chase; he re-forms the column and keeps walking, and they will meet him again a mile closer to Ardenford.
- **He does not command the zombie** (if you're stacking the v2 Calderon soldier in). He tries. He calls it by a name that isn't its name, twice, and it does not stop, and it is the only moment all encounter where he looks frightened.

## Loot

A censer-headed **mace** (mundane — the radiant was *him*, not the weapon), a waterlogged **battalion prayer book**, and the **third company muster roll**: a hundred and twenty-seven years old, still legible, every man in the company listed by name and village. **Corwin Caldor is on it.** Renn Caldor would give a great deal for that page, and the veterans' community in Ardenford rather more.

## Description (one line)

A greying man of about fifty in the mud-stiff grey-and-oak cassock of a Ridgeward battalion chaplain, walking backwards ahead of his column so he can keep an eye on them, cheerfully counting heads that are not there.
