# Stirge

**HP** 5 (2d4) **·** **AC** 13 (natural) **·** **Speed** 10 ft., **fly 40 ft.** **·** **Darkvision** 60 ft. **·** **Passive Perception** 9 **·** **CR** 1/8 (25 XP)

> Action mechanics live in `combat-runner/actions.jsonl` — see the launcher's **Ready actions** reference for verbs.

**They are not on anybody's side.** They are not the smugglers' guard animals and the smugglers cannot control them. They are what has been living in the roof of a warm, wet, dark cave that people keep walking into.

**Identical in all three variants** (see [`_overview.md`](../_overview.md)) — they are terrain, not a faction. In **v2** they are at their best: dropping mid-argument means a frightened woman, four dock-hands and six adventurers all fighting the same thing at once, and nobody can quite get back to where the argument was afterwards.

---

## Start-of-turn checklist

1. **Are they down yet?** If not — **do not roll initiative for them.** They enter mid-fight via **Drop From The Dark** (start of round 2 or 3, or the first torch, or the first real shout). One Stealth roll of **1d20+3** for the whole flight vs. passive Perception; beat it and the first Proboscis has **advantage** on a surprised target.
2. **Is this one attached?** → **Blood Drain**, automatically, right now, at the start of its turn. **2d4 necrotic**, no attack roll, no save. It cannot make attacks while attached.
3. **Has it drained twice, or has it taken any damage?** → **Detach And Flutter.** Gorged ones leave the encounter for good; damaged ones climb out of reach and re-pick.
4. **Otherwise:** it is in the air. **Proboscis** the nearest warm body.

---

## Tactics — when the DM asks "what does it do?"

- **Entry:** they drop. All three at once, from a ceiling nobody has been able to see all fight. The sound arrives before anything else does — a dry leathery flutter directly overhead — and the party gets exactly one beat to look up.
- **Target selection is not tactical.** **Nearest warm body**, every time, and it does not matter whose. A stirge will land on a smuggler as happily as on a paladin. For one round everybody in the cave has the same problem — play that; it is the best beat in the encounter.
- **Latch and drain.** Once attached it stops attacking and just feeds: 2d4 necrotic at the start of each of its turns, automatically, until someone spends an **action** to pull it off. **This is what kills someone at level 1.** Two drains will drop most level-1 casters. Track which stirge is on whom.
- **It stays in the air.** Fly 40 ft, walk 10 ft — it does not land except on a person. It cannot be flanked, and a melee PC with no reach and no bow is going to have a bad round. Reward the player who thinks of a thrown weapon, a torch, or a friend with a shield.
- **An attached stirge is a legal target** — but it is on somebody, and misses and AoE go into the host. Let the table work that out.
- **No morale, because no loyalty.** They are not a faction, they are weather. They do not avenge each other, do not coordinate, and do not retreat as a group.
- **Gorged:** two drains and it is full. It detaches, climbs back into the roof-dark to digest, and **does not come down again**. The encounter can end without a single stirge dying, and the party can simply leave.

## Description (one line)

Something the size of a cat and the shape of nothing that should exist, all wings and legs and a needle where a face ought to be, dropping out of a ceiling that has been twenty-five feet of solid darkness this entire time.
