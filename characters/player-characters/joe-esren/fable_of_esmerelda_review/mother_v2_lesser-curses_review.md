## 2. Disentangled verdicts

The panel was explicitly asked to judge the two changes *separately*. The verdicts cleanly separate.

### Axis A — Does the panel favor "Ear" over "Hearing"?

**YES — unanimous. "Ear" wins on every verdict (10/10) and every steelman that addresses it.**

The recurring reasons, in rough order of how often they appear:

- **Register consistency.** The omen grammar is terse, concrete nouns ("Fear. People."). "Ear. Tongue." is two body parts in one bodily register — a matched anatomical pair. "Hearing. Tongue." yokes an *abstract faculty/gerund* to a *concrete organ*: a category seam the eye snags on.
- **The Voice→Tongue precedent.** "Voice" was already demoted to "Tongue" specifically to stay concrete and dodge the cosmic "the Voice" collision. "Ear" honors that direction; "Hearing" quietly *reverses* it, re-introducing the abstraction the Tongue edit was meant to escape. Several personas call this decisive.
- **Cadence / sound.** Clipped monosyllabic "Ear" matches the struck-bone meter; the soft `-ing` tail of "Hearing" blunts it.
- **Beat-fit.** A matched, frictionless pair lets the reader *skim past* — exactly what a confidence beat wants.

**Only conceded point for "Hearing":** it names the loss slightly more *vividly* — but the panel treats vividness as a *liability* here, since these are lesser curses she swats aside, not curses meant to land.

**Verdict A: favor "Ear." Adopt the body-theme token.**

### Axis B — Does the panel favor ADDING "Disfigurement."?

**NO — unanimous against. All 10 verdicts rank the 2-token variants above their 3-token twins.**

**Crucial nuance for a faithful read of this 5/5 split:** the tally records each persona's *primary* objection, but the split is **not** a disagreement about substance. **Every single verdict raises all three faults** (mismatch, on-the-nose, overload); they differ only on *which is decisive*. The "mismatch" camp and the "overload" camp reach the identical conclusion — cut the third token — and even agree on the fallback (if forced to keep it, use W1 not W3). So the verdict against adding "Disfigurement." is effectively 10/10 by substance, 5/5 only by emphasis.

**Verdict B: do NOT add "Disfigurement." Keep two tokens.**

## 3. Strongest case FOR and AGAINST the disfigurement irony

### Strongest case FOR (steelman, from the ADVOCATE brief)

The "warded as fast as they came" beat is the *only* place in the fable where the
witch treats bodily ruin as beneath notice — so it is the natural, honest place to
**bury** the category of harm she will later inflict on her own child. A spare fable
lives on its plants, and a plant works best when hidden where no one is looking; the
*speed* of the three-beat list is the camouflage. Gains:

- **Irony with teeth** — on reread her casual warding becomes monstrous in hindsight.
- **Characterization by omission** — that she finds "Disfigurement" *trivial* tells us, without exposition, exactly the kind of mother who routes the bargain through her son.
- **Fate, not accident** — it pre-pays the climax's emotional debt so the unmaking reads as foretold.
- **The boldest defense of the mismatch:** reframe the gap as the *engine*, not the flaw — the bones offered her the *smaller* word ("just disfigurement") and she waved it off; the universe answered with the *larger* one (a son erased). The distance between dismissed-harm and delivered-harm *is* the measure of her misjudgment. On this reading, the category mismatch IS the cruelty.

### Strongest case AGAINST (the panel consensus, three converging faults)

- **(a) Mismatch — the decisive one for half the panel.** The son is **unmade**: *"something else lifted its head, and opened its eyes."* That is substitution / possession / transformation — *ontological erasure*, a new being wearing his shape. "Disfigurement" denotes a *marred-but-still-him* body. The harm that destroys him is therefore **not the category she shrugs at**, so the "she dismissed the very harm that doomed him" payoff doesn't click — the key doesn't fit the lock. A reader who reaches back for the seed feels a **near-miss**, which reads as *authorial confusion about what happened to the son* rather than cruel irony. A near-miss seed is worse than no seed.
- **(b) On-the-nose.** "Disfigurement" is the lone abstract, Latinate, multi-syllable noun in a field of bone-blunt monosyllables (Fear, People, Ear, Tongue, Moment, Relive). It *announces* its own thematic function — the prose winking at its own foreshadowing. The best fable seeds stay invisible until reread.
- **(c) Overload — the decisive one for the other half.** The beat's single job is *rising confidence*, hard-capped by *"No price the bones gave was beyond turning."* Two crisp tokens enact the swat-it-away momentum of *"warded as fast as they came."* A heavy abstract third token makes the reader **pause** on the very line that should feel like effortless dispatch. It also breaks a load-bearing formal pattern: the omens come in **twos** elsewhere (*"Fear. People."*, *"Moment. Relive."*), and the un-wardable climax *"Moment. Relive."* lands harder *because* it arrives in the same two-beat meter the wardable omens established. Breaking to three here weakens that setup. Note too that the **next clause already supplies the plurality** — *"The lesser curses, one by one"* — so a third token is structurally redundant with the line that follows it.

**Why FOR loses (neutral assessment):** the advocate's mismatch-as-engine reframe is genuinely clever, but it requires the reader to *consciously reconstruct* an inverted-category irony in retrospect — work the spare register actively resists. Even one persona who *came in wanting* the richer 3-token version (the maximalist-skeptic) concluded that "a maximalist who respects the text has to let the unmaking/disfigurement mismatch veto the very richness he's drawn to." The FOR case is real but rests on an effect the prose is built to suppress.

## 4. Recommendations

### On each change, separately

- **Change 1 — "Hearing" → "Ear": ADOPT.** Unanimous, well-reasoned, and reinforced by the existing Voice→Tongue editorial logic. Two concrete body-part nouns keep the omen grammar in one key.
- **Change 2 — add "Disfigurement.": REJECT.** Unanimous against, on three converging faults (mismatch / on-the-nose / overload). Keep the cast to **two tokens** so the beat stays fast and the confidence reads as momentum.

### Single best variant

**→ W4: "Ear. Tongue."**

Unanimous top pick (10/10). It adopts Change 1, rejects Change 2, and is strictly
better than the current text (W2) on the token-style axis while matching it on count.

### Replacement line (drop-in for line 34)

> Fear. People. The fear of being alone. She drew the ward on her arm in her own red, a guard against isolation. She cast again. **Ear. Tongue.** The lesser curses, one by one — warded as fast as they came. No price the bones gave was beyond turning.

### If the son-irony seed is wanted anyway

The mismatch is real — **the son is unmade, not disfigured**, so "Disfigurement"
points at the wrong category and the irony misfires on reread. State this plainly to
the author. Two paths the panel implied:

1. **Preferred — seed it structurally, not lexically (most-cited).** Her unbroken
   confidence here — *"No price the bones gave was beyond turning"* — is *already* the
   irony engine: it sets up the one price she cannot turn, routed through her child.
   The reader builds that connection unaided; naming a token pre-chews it. This keeps
   W4 and avoids the mismatch entirely.

2. **If a lexical token is mandatory — change the word, not just the count.** Use a
   token in the **unmaking / substitution** family that actually survives the callback,
   kept short and concrete so it doesn't overload the beat. Tokens the panel floated:
   **"Other."**, **"Change."**, **"Unmaking."** — e.g. *"Ear. Tongue. Other."* — so the
   later *"something else lifted its head"* lands as a **snapped thread** rather than a
   near-miss. "Disfigurement" specifically should not be used.
   - And if the author insists on "Disfigurement" *as written*, the least-bad carrier is
     **W1 (Ear. Tongue. Disfigurement.)**, never W3 — keep the two consistent body-part
     nouns so the abstraction at least sits among bodily kin. But W1 still ranks below
     both two-token variants in 9 of 10 verdicts.

### Bottom line

Adopt **"Ear."**; drop **"Disfigurement."**; ship **W4 — "Ear. Tongue."** The irony
the author wants is worth keeping, but it belongs in the *structure* (the unwardable
price) or on a *different token* (the unmaking category) — not crammed into the
confidence beat as a word that mis-predicts the ending.
