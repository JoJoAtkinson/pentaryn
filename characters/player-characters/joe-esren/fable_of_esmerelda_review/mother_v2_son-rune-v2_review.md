# Son-Rune v2 — Best PAIR of Bone-Runes (Expanded Pool)

*Neutral synthesis of a 12-persona panel ranking 18 candidate bone-rune pairs. The runes foreshadow a boy becoming a wolf-like beast, cast as a witch's bone-omen. Faithful to the panel; splits reported where they exist.*

---

## (0) Creature ARCHETYPE the research settled on

**An arrested, one-way therianthrope — the *becoming*, frozen mid-rise.** Not a clean two-state shapeshifter but a layered composite: a cursed loup-garou wearing a skinwalker's glowing eyes, a wild-man's kept human face, and a Gévaudan body — a punishment-transformation in which the boy stays conscious and lucid, stranded at the crown while the fur rises over him from below. The defining, species-free signature is **direction: it climbs UP, the human is the last dry land on top.**

## (1) Top-pick distribution + aggregate ranking of the 18

**Top-pick tally (n=12 personas):** P1 "Hunger. Fours." = 7 · P14 "Below. Rises." = 5. No other rune took a single first-place vote. The field is a clean two-horse race for the *most-loved* single rune, with the panel split almost exactly down the middle on a single fault line (see below).

**The fault line driving the split:** Test 1's carved/tenseless register. Personas who read the exemplar "Moment. **Relive.**" as licensing only *bare* verb forms treat every inflected `-s` verb (Bends, Climbs, Walks, Sings, Doubles, Outgrows, Joins, **Rises**) as a disqualifying narration-fault — these vote **P1** and push P14 down to mid-pack. Personas who hear "Rises" as a *gnomic/carved* present that "sits flat on the bone" forgive it and vote **P14** for capturing the becoming's thesis. This is the single largest source of disagreement in the panel.

## (2) The WINNING PAIR and runners-up — how each passes the three tests

The brief asks for the best **pair**. A strong pair must (a) draw both members from the panel's top tier and (b) cover **complementary** axes — two runes that hint different facets of the becoming rather than redundantly saying the same thing. Scoring candidate pairs by summed aggregate rank plus a redundancy check:

| Pair | Members | Sum of means | Note |
|------|---------|--------------|------|
| **P1 + P13** | Hunger. Fours. / Crown. Spared. | **6.92** | **gait/hunger × trapped-self — fully complementary** |
| P8 + P13 | Two. One-Lit. / Crown. Spared. | 6.92 | eyes × trapped-self — complementary |
| P1 + P8 | Hunger. Fours. / Two. One-Lit. | 7.00 | gait × eyes — complementary |
| P13 + P14 | Crown. Spared. / Below. Rises. | 9.00 | trapped-self × direction — strong but near-synonymous (both "top stays, beast rises") |
| P1 + P14 | Hunger. Fours. / Below. Rises. | 9.08 | gait × direction |

### WINNER — **P1 "Hunger. Fours." + P13 "Crown. Spared."**

Lowest summed aggregate (tied 6.92) **and** maximally complementary: one rune carves the *beast rising from below* (drive + quadruped gait), the other carves the *human stranded at the top* (the false-mercy sting). Between them they encode the whole curve of the curse — the becoming and its cruelty — with zero overlap and zero animal noun. The tie-break over P8+P13 goes to P1 because it carries the *becoming itself* (a body re-counting its limbs) where P8 carries only a *feature* (the eyes); pairing the trapped-self with the transformation beats pairing it with a tell.

- **P1 "Hunger. Fours." — Test 1 (carved/tenseless):** PASS, strongest in the field. Two bare nouns in the exact mold of "Fear. People."; "Fours" is a noun-rune (a count, not a verb), so it carries **no `-s` inflection fault** — the very fault that sinks P14 for half the panel. This is why P1, not P14, is the safe anchor. **Test 2 (specific wolf-becoming):** PASS. Predatory hunger fused to the drop onto all-fours = a human gait failing into a beast's, the wolf implied and never named. (Minor dissent: two personas note the creature is hunched-upright/gangly-armed rather than clearly four-footed, a slight image-mismatch — see alternatives.) **Test 3 (hint not describe):** PASS. Opaque on the cast (four *what*?), detonates on payoff when the boy goes down on all fours; trips no banned word.
- **P13 "Crown. Spared." — Test 1:** PASS. "Spared" is a carved past-participle, not an inflected present-tense verb; reads as struck-on-bone. (One persona flags it as a *softer* participle fault than a clean noun, costing it a notch on the strictest readings — but it never falls below 8th anywhere.) **Test 2:** PASS by inversion — the human top "shall remain," the precise cruelty of *this* curse (spared only to stay awake inside the rising beast). Dissent: it evokes the lupine becoming *by implication*; a generic curse could also spare a head, so a few personas dock its species-specificity. **Test 3:** PASS, the purest hint in the field — names no body part and no animal, reads as MERCY first and HORROR second.

### Runner-up 1 — **P8 "Two. One-Lit." + P13 "Crown. Spared."** (sum 6.92)

The other tied-lowest pair, and the *consensus-safest* of all (P8 + P13 are the two tightest-spread runes in the panel — neither ever ranks worse than 8th). **P8 Test 1:** PASS — bone-terse compound, no inflected verb. **Test 2:** the mismatched glowing eye (one turquoise, lit in the dark), the skinwalker "you know them by the eyes" tell — eerily specific, but a *feature*, not the whole becoming. **Test 3:** PASS, very cryptic, never names the creature. Pairs cleanly with P13's trapped-self (eyes are literally the rune of the captive self). Chosen as runner-up over the winner only because it foreshadows two *tells* (eye + spared crown) rather than the *transformation* itself.

### Runner-up 2 — **P1 "Hunger. Fours." + P8 "Two. One-Lit."** (sum 7.00)

Two of the three most carved, register-clean runes in the field, covering gait × eyes. **Both pass Test 1 cleanly** (the register-strict half of the panel's two favorites). Strong if you want *two* unimpeachable carved omens and are willing to leave the trapped-self theme implicit. Slightly less thematically deep than any P13 pairing because it foreshadows two physical tells without the moral/emotional sting of "he shall remain."

### Runner-up 3 — **P13 "Crown. Spared." + P14 "Below. Rises."** (sum 9.00)

The "thesis pair" — direction-of-becoming × trapped-self, the two runes that most directly state the curse's central image. Thematically the richest of all and beloved by the register-lenient personas (folklorist, close-reader, poetry editor, TTRPG reader all rank both top-3). **Disqualified from winning on two counts:** (a) P14 carries the `-s` inflection fault that half the panel treats as fatal — pairing it removes the safety P1 provides; (b) P13 and P14 are **near-synonymous** ("the beast rises / the top stays human" is one idea split across two bones), so the pair is redundant rather than complementary. Use only if you prize maximum thematic on-the-nose-ness over register-safety and breadth.

## (3) Recommendation

**Cast these two runes: "Hunger. Fours." and "Crown. Spared." (P1 + P13).**

Rationale: it is the lowest-aggregate pair, it is fully complementary (the rising beast × the stranded boy — the becoming and its cruelty), and critically **P1 sidesteps the one fault that fractured the panel** — the `-s` inflection that costs P14 half its support. You get one register-bulletproof carved omen (P1) anchoring one of the most thematically devastating (P13), with no animal noun, no banned word, and no redundancy between the two bones.

**Alternatives, in order:**

1. **Maximum-safety / consensus:** swap P1 → **P8 "Two. One-Lit."** for **P8 + P13**. Tied on aggregate, and these are the two *least-polarizing* runes in the entire field (tightest rank-spreads). Choose this if you want the pair least likely to read wrong to any single reader — at the cost of foreshadowing two tells rather than the transformation. It also answers P1's lone weakness (the creature isn't clearly quadruped) by leaning on the unambiguous eye-tell instead.
2. **Two clean carved tells:** **P1 + P8** (gait × eyes). Use if you want both runes register-bulletproof and are content to leave the trapped-self theme for the prose to carry.
3. **Maximum theme, accept the register risk:** **P13 + P14** (the "thesis pair"). The richest, most on-image pairing — pick it only if you (a) read "Rises" as carved-gnomic rather than inflected and (b) don't mind the two bones restating one idea.

## (4) Did the expanded pool beat the prior winner "Fur. Climbs."?

**Yes — decisively, and on two independent grounds.**

- **Register (Test 1).** "Fur. Climbs." carries the inflected `-s` verb "Climbs," the exact fault the strict half of the panel treats as disqualifying (it sinks P3 "Heel. Climbs." to a mean of 7.9 for precisely this reason). The recommended winner **P1 "Hunger. Fours." is register-clean** — two bare nouns, no verb to inflect — so it is strictly safer than the prior winner on the criterion that most divides expert readers.
- **Hint vs. describe (Test 3).** "Fur." is a **bare body-sheet noun for the animal's coat** — it names the thing the rune is supposed to hide. The word-bank flags raw pelt/fur nouns as ★ "almost-names-it" words to be used late and sparingly, not as a primary cast. Every member of the recommended pair (Hunger / Fours / Crown / Spared) names **no** animal part: they hint by drive, by count, by the spared human top. The expanded pool replaced a rune that *describes* with runes that *point*.

The expansion also surfaced the panel's true center of gravity — the consensus top tier **P13/P8/P1/P14** — none of which is "Fur. Climbs." The prior winner would not rank inside the top tier of this larger field: it shares P3's inflection fault and adds an explicit-coat-noun fault P3 doesn't have. The expanded search beat it.
