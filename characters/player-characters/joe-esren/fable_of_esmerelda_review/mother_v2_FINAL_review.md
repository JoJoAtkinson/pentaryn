## The one real decision — legibility vs. Kafkan withholding (line 71, and the rule-statements)

This is the only item worth your deliberation. Everything else is optional or noise.

**The tradition benchmark's top gap (8–9 of 10 panelists):** the fable *states* rules and moral that the ending was built to *disclose* —
- the rule-statements at **L29 / L41 / L45 / L47** ("to ask the Voice was to be cursed for the asking"; "the innocent paid nothing… Untaught, he was safe"; "to teach him was to make the asking hers, and the price his");
- the tribunal **gloss at L67 / L71** ("our children are tokens"; "not a curse you took it for, but your ask" — *an elder decoding the plot for the reader*).
The benchmark's recommendation: cut the gloss, let the recurrence of "Moment. Relive." carry the disclosure. *Kafka never explains the Law.*

**Why this is a FORK, not a fix — and where your own panels collide:**
- Your **22-agent reveal panel** ruled **keep both** the elder's L71 reveal *and* Esmerelda's interior (9/10 effective-as-is, 0 to collapse, all 4 blind rankers ranked the current text #1). It judged the doubling *the thesis enacted* — the truth spoken aloud, then taken inside.
- Your **tradition benchmark** names that same L71 line as *the top thing holding the piece below peer level.*
- **Both are right within their frame.** The reveal panel optimized for *this* fable (legible, fair, emotionally doubled); the benchmark optimized for *peer-with-Kafka* (maximal withholding).

**The crucial nuance the benchmark itself surfaces:** your "over-explanation" is partly the **fair-play** the fable deliberately chose — and the benchmark *praises* it for it: the twist is *planted* (the un-turnable bones at L37), so the reveal is an *earned re-reading*, which **"exceeds many Kafka imitators in fairness."** Kafka can spring the doorkeeper's line *because he doesn't play fair*. So cutting the gloss toward Kafka would trade **fairness + emotional clarity** for **opacity**. That's a different fable, not a better one.

**My read:** keep L71 (your reveal panel was thorough and right *for the fable you're writing*). **But** there may be a *surgical* middle path that gains a half-step toward the exemplar bar without losing fairness: of the mid-piece rule-statements, **L47 ("What was one curse against a life without his sister?")** is the most cuttable — it's interiority that softens Esmerelda toward sympathy, and Carter would "let the act stand colder." L29/41/45 are load-bearing for fair-play (the polish panel credits L29 as the *irony engine*), so leave them. **Net: this is your call, and it's a question of which fable you want — not a defect to repair.**
