**Sharp single lines / devices**

**Tone control**

## MEDIUM — genuine splits & contested (your call)

### `knelt next to her daughter` sits a half-register below the elevated diction around it
- **Location:** Line 3, sentence 2
- **6-vote split:** 3 real_problem / 3 false_positive / 0 tossup — a true 3-3 deadlock.
- **The case for changing it (3 validators):** The clause is elevated on both flanks — "In silence" (formal inversion), "knelt" (elevated over "kneeled"), funereal "laid out on a bed of fallen needles." Against that, "next to" is the one everyday-compound preposition, carrying a faint contemporary-conversational flatness ("sit next to me"). Decisively, the author already reaches for the literary register-mate at the *mirrored* tableau — Line 27, "She set the pruning blade down **beside** her" — and the fable's native idiom for proximity is "beside / at" throughout ("at her shoulder," "at his mother's ear," "to her left"). "Next to" appears exactly once, at the highest-visibility opening human image. Cadence also tightens: "knelt beside her daughter" elides where "knelt next to" clusters the hard /kst-t/. Surgical fix: change "knelt next to her daughter" to "knelt beside her daughter" — one word, zero cost, matches the fable's own register-word at the identical later gesture.
- **The case for leaving it (3 validators):** The controlling register of *sentence 2* is plain, not elevated — the elevated "life-smothering shroud" diction belongs to sentence 1 (the forest); sentence 2 deliberately steps DOWN into intimate, monosyllabic diction ("knelt," "her daughter," "laid out," "bed of fallen needles"). That grandeur→intimacy descent is a craft move the whole fable repeats (the bare "Bring her back to me," "my lovely son"). "Next to" matches the plain baseline cleanly; nothing snags on a real first read. Worse, the author demonstrably *reserves* "beside" for the Line 27 settling beat, so swapping it into Line 3 creates an unwanted echo and erases the textural distinction between the two kneeling scenes.
- **The tradeoff:** Both sides agree "beside" is euphonious and in-voice. They disagree on whether sentence 2's register is plain-by-design (so "next to" fits) or elevated-by-flank (so "next to" dips). The change is a defensible sharpening OR a defensible no-op that risks an echo with Line 27.
- **Unresolved.** Author's call. If you change it, accept the faint "beside" echo at Line 27; if you keep it, you are crediting the deliberate plain-register descent.
