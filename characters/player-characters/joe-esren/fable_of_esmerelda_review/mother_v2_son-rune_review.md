# Son-Rune Review — Synthesis

**Brief:** Find the best PAIR of bone-runes to foreshadow a boy becoming a beast.
**Register canon:** the fable's own cast tokens — `Fear. People.` / `Ear. Tongue.` / `Moment. Relive.` (terse, concrete, two capitalized beats, cryptic now and detonating only at payoff).
**Three tests:** (1) reads-as-rune — matches the bone-cast cadence; (2) evokes-transformation — lands as a body becoming animal; (3) hints-not-describes — never names the creature or its anatomy outright.

## 2. The Winning Pair and the Runner-Up

### WINNER — P8: "Fur. Climbs."

- **Reads-as-rune:** Exact structural twin of `Moment. Relive.` — concrete body-noun + one bare present-tense verb, two stressed beats, no articles, no abstraction. The single most in-register pair in the set; multiple personas call its cadence a perfect match.
- **Evokes-transformation:** This is the brief's central image rendered as grammar. "Climbs" makes the fur an *active agent* ascending the body from below — flesh being overtaken in motion, a process not a mood. It stealth-encodes the canon mechanic exactly: *fur grown up over him from below, only the top still human.* Maximal becoming-animal payload.
- **Hints-not-describes:** Names no creature — no wolf, beast, hound, or monster — and avoids the reserved verb "Turn." "Climbs" withholds both the agent and the outcome, misdirecting toward something innocuous (ivy, an ascent) on first read; only at payoff does the reader see fur rising over a boy. Cryptic now, devastating in retrospect.
- **The one objection (reported):** the test-3 purist holds that "Fur" is a noun lifted off the creature sheet and so leaks the result. The majority answer: "Fur" is a *covering/substance*, not a beast-name, and the verb "Climbs" is what carries the meaning and keeps the agent withheld — so the pair hints rather than describes.

### RUNNER-UP — P5: "Heel. Lifts."

- **Reads-as-rune:** Near-perfect bone-cast cadence — bare noun + intransitive verb, the same two clean beats as the winner. Repeatedly called a "true rune."
- **Evokes-transformation:** Cleanly encodes the digitigrade reveal — the heel rising off the ground as the foot re-architects into a beast's. A real body-becoming-animal signal.
- **Hints-not-describes:** Gloriously cryptic now — a lifting heel reads as innocuous tiptoe or a stumble — and names nothing. The faithful caveat: it is a *narrower* signal (one feature, not the whole body) and subtle enough that some readers may miss the becoming-animal charge until payoff. That narrowness is the only thing keeping it behind P8.

---

## 3. Recommendation

**Use the pair: `Fur. Climbs.` and `Heel. Lifts.`**

- **`Fur. Climbs.` (P8)** is the lead rune — the panel's near-unanimous winner, the brief's core image made grammar, and the cleanest match to the fable's `Moment. Relive.` cadence. It does the heavy lifting: whole-body becoming, devastating on reveal, names nothing.
- **`Heel. Lifts.` (P5)** is the ideal partner. It pairs without redundancy: where "Fur. Climbs." gives the *covering* rising over the whole body, "Heel. Lifts." gives a *single architectural* change (the digitigrade foot) — a second, quieter bone in a different register of the same transformation. Two complementary withholdings, not two versions of one image.

### Strongest alternatives

1. **`Spine. Bows.` (P7)** — flawless cadence and a vivid bodily change (the permanent hunch). Swap it in for P5 if you want a more *visible*, posture-level signal. Caveat the panel flags: "Bows" can misread as submission or age before payoff.
2. **`Smile. Snarl.` (P4)** — the wildcard. It is the panel's most polarizing pair (one 1st-place vote, otherwise bottom-third) but its champions make a real case: it uniquely weaponizes the fable's load-bearing **smile** motif between mother and son, names no anatomy, and detonates on payoff. The majority objection is that a snarl is *affect/behavior*, not flesh changing (weak on test 2), and that poaching the reserved smile risks muddying it. Consider it only if you specifically want the rune to echo the smile thread — and accept it trades transformation-charge for that echo.

**Avoid** the describer-pairs that half-name the result — `Skin. Pelt.`, `Hand. Claw.`, `Fang. Crowds.` — and `Boy. Litter.`, which names the victim and points at offspring/pack rather than his own body transforming.
