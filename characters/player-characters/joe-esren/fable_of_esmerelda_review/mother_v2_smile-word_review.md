# Smile-word review — "Her smile ___."

*Neutral synthesis of a 10-panelist review. The slot is the fable's moral pivot: a maternal smile inverting into the decision to spend her living son.*

**Candidates:** soured · hardened · turned · cooled · sharpened · curdled
**Currently in the slot:** **hardened**

## 2. Where "hardened" actually placed

**hardened finished 4th of 6** on aggregate (mean rank 3.70), with **one first-place vote** and **one last-place vote** — the only word besides curdled to be ranked both best and worst by some panelist.

Its ballot spread (1,2,3,3,4,4,4,5,5,6) shows the divide cleanly: the prose-stylist and the two "skeptic" readers who treat the slot as a **decision beat** rank it high (1st–3rd); the readers who treat the slot as a **corruption / spoilage beat** push it to 4th–6th, because "hardness reads as resolve/steel, not rot," and one reader (reader-response) judged it *almost sympathetic* — "grit" that softens rather than darkens the pivot, which is why they ranked it dead last.

So: hardened is a defensible mid-tier choice with a real, articulable case — but it is **not** where the weight of the panel sits.

---

## 3. The strongest case FOR each — hardened vs. its leading rival

The leading rival is **curdled** (plurality winner, aggregate winner). Here is the best version of each argument.

### Strongest case FOR "hardened"

Read the slot as a **decision beat, not an imagery beat.** The sentence's own logic is resolve: *"Her smile ___. He could help, all he had to do was ask."* That second clause is a settled plan — cold, practical, already half-executed. Five of the six candidates (sour, curdle, cool, sharpen, turn) describe the smile going *bad* — a change of flavor or temperature. **hardened** is the only one that describes the *woman going decided*. It borrows the idiom "her resolve hardened," so it records the **choice** (to spend the son) that is the actual pivot of the fable.

Supporting strengths the steelman raises:
- **Tightest antonym for the triad.** The first smile is "meek and breaking" — pliant, yielding. Its exact opposite is not "rotten" but **rigid**. Breaking-softness answered by hardening is a cleaner rule-of-three than an imagery swap.
- **Agency-bearing cold.** It lands the warm→cold pole *with* will (cool is cold but passive; hardened is cold *and* willed), prefiguring the "impersonal judgment / eternal loop" ending where everything has set hard.
- **Cleanest reserved-verb safety.** Zero collision with the warding verb "turn," and — unlike "sharpened" — it borrows no edge/rune imagery from the witch's machinery either.
- **Most restrained.** Two plain Anglo-Saxon syllables; it withholds the *how*, matching prose that refuses to over-specify.

Honest weakness (conceded by its own advocate): hardness reads as steel/resolve more than corruption, so it slightly **underplays the moral-horror inversion** the triad wants — and to one reader it tipped toward sympathetic grit.

### Strongest case FOR "curdled" (the rival)

**curdled is the only candidate that encodes the warm→cold pole as an *event* rather than a state: you cannot curdle what was never warm.** The word silently carries the maternal afternoon-warmth and spoils it *from within* — which is precisely the fable's actual subject, maternal love souring into the willingness to spend a child. It doubles the whole warm-to-cold pole inside one verb: it begins in the warm domain (milk, an afternoon, a mother) and ends in the cold one (rot, the transaction).

Supporting strengths:
- **Cleanest sinister inversion of the triad** (grief-smile → innocent-smile → corrupted-smile): the same maternal tenderness, gone bad, rather than a swap to a new abstract quality or a different image.
- **Shows without telling.** It never names the calculation ("she would spend her son"); it shows spoilage and trusts the reader — suiting an understated fable, and licensed because this is the *single pivot* where lean prose may spend vividness.
- **No reserved-verb collision.** It means "spoiled" while leaving "turn" entirely intact for the wards and the curse.

Honest weakness (conceded by skeptics): it is the **most writerly / loudest** of the six, the one most at risk of *over-specifying* an affect the spare prose wants left to inference — and it imports a faint milk-rot *disgust* that two readers felt was the wrong temperature for cold calculation.

**The hinge between them** is a question of what the slot *is*: a **decision** (→ hardened) or a **corruption** (→ curdled). Seven of ten panelists read it as corruption-in-place, which is why curdled and its twin soured dominate the top of the ballot.

---

## 4. The RESERVED-TURN question — does "turned" collide with the warding verb?

**The panel's conclusion is unanimous and unambiguous: YES, it collides, and the collision matters enough to disqualify "turned."**

All ten ballots rank **turned** in the bottom three; **seven of ten rank it dead last**, and the remaining three (which place it 2nd or 3rd) do so *only* on pure local fitness while still flagging the collision as a real demerit.

The shared reasoning:

- In this short, spare fable, **"turn" is a near-technical warding term** — the witch's runes "turn" a curse, and the climax hinges on the one curse that **"would not turn."** Scarcity is what gives that word its charge.
- In compressed prose the ear *pools* repeated key words, so "Her smile turned" reads as an intentional echo. It does damage in **both directions**: it (a) prematurely rings the warding-bell / plants a false foreshadow the reader tries to cash in, and (b) dilutes "turn" into ordinary usage so the climactic "would not turn" loses its reserved charge.
- Critically, the panel notes this is a demotion on **cross-reference cost, not local quality.** Several readers concede "Her smile turned" is, in isolation, a perfectly good — even the sparest and most idiomatic — sinister-pivot phrase. It loses *purely* because an equally-good non-colliding word exists, so there is no reason to pay the cost.

**Bottom line on the reserved-turn question: avoid "turned." Keep "turn" in reserve for the wards and the curse.** This is the single point of full panel agreement.

## 5. Recommendation

### Verdict: **switch off "hardened" — but the panel is split on the target.**

hardened is not *wrong* — it finished a legitimate 4th with a coherent decision-beat case, and it is safe on every structural test (pole, register, reserved verb). But it sits below the panel's center of gravity, and its specific failure mode is real: **it reads as resolve/steel rather than the corruption of maternal warmth that this exact pivot turns on.** The fable's subject is love going bad, not a will setting; "hardened" names the wrong half of that.

The panel does not converge on one replacement, so here are the two defensible moves, in order of support.

#### Primary recommendation: **curdled**

The plurality winner (5 firsts) and aggregate winner (mean 2.10). Choose it if you accept that this slot is the licensed place to spend one vivid image.

- **Pole:** carries the warm→cold axis *inside one verb* — curdling requires prior warmth, so the cold note lands as corruption *of* the warm afternoon, not a separate temperature. It seeds the frozen, impersonal-judgment ending from inside the warmest scene.
- **Triad:** the cleanest sinister inversion of grief-smile → innocent-smile → corrupted-smile; the same maternal tenderness spoiling in place, with the milk/mother resonance doubling the theme (a mother spending her child).
- **Register:** shows the spoilage without naming the calculation — fits prose that won't over-specify — *but* this is its one risk: it is the loudest word in the set, and if the surrounding prose is very bare, it can feel performed.

#### Fallback / safest recommendation: **soured**

If you want curdled's exact mechanism (warm thing going off, in the same organic key as the first two smiles) with **less risk of over-writing**, switch to **soured** instead. It is the panel's true consensus pick: second on aggregate (2.40), the *most stable* ballot of any word (never ranked worse than 4th), and curdled's "leaner, quieter twin." It keeps the third smile biological and human — a corruption of the same substance, not a swap to steel — and it sidesteps "turn" cleanly. Its only knock is mildness: it can read as mere displeasure rather than moral taint, so it under-delivers where curdled over-delivers.

- **The minimalist's argument**, worth weighing for this register: in a fable that refuses to over-specify, *plainness is the correct register for the pivot* — soured does the most work with the least gesture and distrusts the showier synonym.

#### If you stay on the cold pole instead: **cooled**

The three structural/skeptic readers who led with **cooled** make a coherent case: it is the only *literal temperature* word, so it doesn't merely foreshadow the frozen ending — it *is* that ending in miniature, the first dropped degree. It is the most understated option and reserved-verb-clean. The cost the rest of the panel charges it: it reads as **withdrawal/distance** (warmth leaving) rather than the *active* corruption of leaning toward the child — and it shares hardened's problem of muting the spoilage. Pick it only if you want to privilege the thermal axis above the corruption beat.

---

### One-line summary

Drop **hardened** (4th of 6 — it lands resolve, not the corruption the pivot needs). Go to **curdled** for maximum thematic punch (panel plurality + aggregate winner; warm-milk-gone-bad doubles the mother/cold theme), or to **soured** for the same mechanism at lower register-risk (the consensus-safe runner-up). **Do not** use **turned** — the panel unanimously confirms it poaches the reserved warding verb and dilutes the "would not turn" climax.
