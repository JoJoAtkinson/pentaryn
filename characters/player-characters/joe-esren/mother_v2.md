
# Fable of Esmerelda  

The ancient, flourishing pine forest of Elderholt cast a life-smothering shroud over the dead floor below. In a silence that swallowed her whole, Esmerelda knelt next to her daughter, laid out on a bed of fallen needles.

"Wake her up." 

It was her son standing at her shoulder. His words carried no further than his mother's ear. "You're allowed to wake people up."

Twice she told him his sister was gone. Still, he did not understand. "I'll show you," he said. "I can wake her." And he got on his knees and shook her shoulder. "It's time to wake up. Don't you want to play?"

She smiled at him, meek and breaking. Her son smiled back, and it dawned on her that he was not wrong. Her smile turned. He could help, all he had to do was ask. 

"Let's play a game," she told him. "Not out loud, love. Inside." She pressed his hand flat to his chest. "Feel that? That's where it lives. Say hello." 

"Hello!" he said.

She gave a half-hearted chuckle. "Yes, love, again — but this time, from the inside." 

And there, in all that emptiness, they practiced. And practiced. They practiced the way you teach a child please and thank you — until she said: now. Ask your inside voice to wake her.

---

That afternoon, the cool air rolled in off the unseen sea as Esmerelda used a pruning blade to work the herb beds, a tangle of growths meant for brews no hearth would want. Her son was helping by digging holes for nothing. 

Then something tore loose. Not sound — a wrongness. The quiet whispers, always there, all at once now. Screaming. Pulling her deeper past the beds, past the tended ground to where her daughter had gone alone. 

Esmerelda found her where the whispers stopped. She knelt, knowing, and still her hand went to her daughter's cheek, as if it might be wrong. She set the pruning blade down beside her and said it aloud, to no one, to the tree: "Bring her back to me." And again: "Bring her back to me." Not to the Voice. Not yet. 

Esmerelda shook her head. The words aloud had cost nothing, and nothing was what they would buy. To ask the Voice was to be cursed for the asking. Every witch knew that much, and to ward a curse, you first had to _see_ it: to look ahead and read the price before it came due. 

Pushing the needles aside, she bared a small circle of ground and drew a set of inscribed bones. She cast them without hesitation.

Fear. People. The fear of being alone. She drew the ward on her arm in her own red, a guard against isolation. She cast again. Hearing. Voice. The lesser curses, one by one — warded as fast as they came. It was working. She could do this.

Again she threw. 

Moment. Relive. To live one moment over, without end. She set the runes to turn it. She cast once more, to be certain. The same. Moment. Relive. Again. Again. They would not change. She did not understand why. 

"Wake her up."

It was her son. She pulled her sleeve down over the fresh wards before he could see. He bore no thread — but the Voice heard the innocent, and the innocent paid nothing. Untaught, he was safe.

As her son shook his sister's body, she willed it through him: _don't ask aloud; ask it, ask the Voice._ He only spoke aloud, the way she had, and the words bought nothing. He could not find the Voice on his own. He would have to be taught.

And to teach him was to make the asking hers, and the price his.

What was one curse against a life without his sister? _His sister would be there to help him through it_, she told herself.

"Hello!" he said.

She gave a half-hearted chuckle. _my lovely son_ — and then, _No._ The boy would have his sister, his sister would help him through whatever the price was. Esmerelda would not let him stay safe. 

The boy reached for the quiet inside, the way she had taught him. He only wanted to help. The Voice answered. Her daughter drew breath. Where the boy had knelt, something else opened its eyes. 

---

Esmerelda stood in the center of the Axiom, a dome-like structure under the elder tree where thoughts are louder then voice. Around her sat the Elders of her coven, each with their own form from a time long before her. 

She did not speak: no hiding what It became, the crime well understood, the punishment well established.  

The Judge of the coven was an old, pale, weathered woman with jagged wards covering every wrinkle. The Judge spoke: "It will need to be dealt with."

The elder to her left, spoke, a misshapen patchwork of absorbed curses, hardly a woman, hardly a *single* monster gave a warm smile. "The boy is self-aware — he shall remain."

A nod passed around the room, one elder to the next, unhurried. The Judge last.

From the shadows, or the shadows themselves "The punishment must deter; or our children are tokens." 

Esmerelda could feel a silent anger sweep over the elders. Here, even stillness had a sound. 

"Moment. Relive." The words come abruptly from an unmarked elder, to no one in particular. "The bones did not lie. You foretold the punishment — not a curse you took it for, but your ask."

Realization struck Esmerelda, _my lovely son, I had warded the price. I had only to bear it myself_. Her legs gave out as grief swept over her.

The room silent now. 

Esmerelda knew the coven ways. She did not protest. She did not wait for judgement. She said it aloud first — to no one, to the tree: "I ask the price be mine." The words cost nothing. They never did.

Then again, this time from the inside. She pressed her own hand flat to her chest, the way she had pressed his. 

_I ask the price be mine._

---

In the forest where the crime was committed, a shadow of Esmerelda relives the moment, again and again, for all time.

*my lovely son.*
