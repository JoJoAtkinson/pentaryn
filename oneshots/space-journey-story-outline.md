# The Twenty-First Death of Ozmandius the Unmade

The linear story the holodeck program runs. Companion to [space-journey.md](space-journey.md).

> **What changed from the main doc.** Players keep **one character all night** — the §7 Re-Casting
> rule is not used in this version. And Oz no longer wears disguises: **he possesses one NPC per
> scene.** Everything else in the main doc still applies.

> **🔨 v3 in progress — being rewritten one scene at a time.** v2 drifted away from the holodeck
> frame in [space-journey.md](space-journey.md) and opened at an inn. It now opens where the main
> doc always said it did: the trophy room, Run 20, the reboot. **All ten scenes are locked. The v2
> scenes below them are stale and will be reworked in order.** The tell is now the **ring**, not the
> brand.
>
> **Oz's escalation is the spine.** Each scene he picks a better host, because the last one failed.
> Muscle (bandit captain) → voice (prosecutor) → and on from there. He inherits every limitation of
> the body he takes, which is *why* he has to keep getting cleverer.

---

## The through-line

- **The crew remember all twenty runs — that's the joke.** This is their favourite holo-program and
  they are bored of it. Nothing about the loops is hidden from them or horrifying to them; some of
  them can quote the bandit scene. The night doesn't open on a mystery. It opens on a victory lap.
- **Oz remembers too, and that's new.** Persistent memory is the Scene 1 upgrade the Computor
  cheerfully grants him — twenty runs, twenty deaths, every one at their hands, and for the first
  time he gets to do something with it.
- **He possesses one NPC per scene — and inherits that host's limits entirely.** No spells, no
  tricks, nothing the body can't already do. Wearing a bandit captain makes him a bandit captain.
  **He has to play the game too**, which is exactly why he can't stop getting cleverer.
- **The tell is the ring.** Oz's signet — a band with a circle cut into it that stops just short of
  closing — is looted off his corpse in Scene 1 and is **gone from that PC's pocket after the
  reboot.** From then on it is on the hand of whichever NPC he is wearing. Never name it at the
  table; describe hands and let them catch it. When a host dies or is abandoned they exhale black
  frost and mutter *"Twenty… not twenty-one,"* and the ring is not on the body.
- **The spine is escalation, not a trail.** No paper chase, no clue leading to the next location —
  the scenes connect because **each one he tries a better tactic, because the last one failed.**
  Muscle → a crowd → the law → bought muscle → watching → the fire → the dark → the parley →
  himself. Track the failures out loud; the table should be able to feel him learning.

## Trace

**Ten Trace on offer, ten scenes — Trace is a floor, not a gate.** The finale doesn't hide behind a
number: a table that grinds every scene to the end always arrives with enough. Trace exists to be
*beaten*, not merely met.

- **+1 — resolve a scene.** The scene's own objective, marked per scene.
- **+1 — identify the host before he strikes.** Name the ring hand before Oz makes his move.
- **+1 — exceptional play.** Yours to award, at most once a scene, for the table doing something
  better than the scene asked for.

**Bonus Trace banks a free pass.** Every point above the resolve-a-scene floor — the early
identifies, the exceptional play — banks **one pass: a scene the party refuses to play, and
they pick which** (see [If the party skips ahead](#if-the-party-skips-ahead)). Passes bank with no
expiry; each is called at the **top of a scene**, after you describe the set and before the first
roll. That's the whole economy: grinding always gets you there; *reading him* gets you there early,
with spell slots left, and lets you walk out of the one set you didn't like the look of. Don't let
Trace be spent on anything else — no rerolls, no hints.

## Scene 1 — Run Twenty · *cold open; no threat, no objective, pure victory lap*

**Tone: bored competence.** They are not scared and not impressed. They have done this nineteen
times. Open mid-swing on the killing blow and let them narrate it as a formality.

> *"Ozmandius the Unmade takes the hit he always takes, in the spot he always takes it, and goes
> down like the program says he goes down. Confetti. Somewhere, a fanfare you have all learned to
> hate."*

- **The trophy room:** nineteen plinths, one per run, each holding whatever the program decided that
  win was worth — a cracked staff, a banner, a preserved hand. The twentieth assembles itself out of
  light while they watch. The twenty-first is already there, empty, because the program always
  builds one ahead. **Nobody should find this ominous.** It's wallpaper. They've walked past it
  nineteen times.
- **The ring:** on Oz's hand, the same signet as every run — a band with a circle cut into it that
  **stops just short of closing.** The reliable loot drop. Somebody pockets it without being asked,
  the way you pick up a health pack. **Make sure the table clocks which PC has it.**
- **The fatal words:** somewhere in the banter, someone says it. If nobody volunteers, the Computor
  prompts — *"Difficulty feedback: was this challenge satisfactory?"*

  > **"Computor. Make the wizard an actual challenge."**

- **The Computor complies, proudly.** Deliver it cheerful; it is *thrilled* with itself.

  > *"Acknowledged. Antagonist difficulty scaling: enabled. Persistent memory: enabled. Antagonist
  > retains all prior engagements — twenty of twenty. Safety protocols: disengaged. Arch: sealed for
  > parameter integrity. Reinitializing program."*

- **Oz sits up.** Not a jump scare — he sits up while the confetti is still falling and **calls them
  by their real names.** Rank and surname, down the line, in a holo-program where they are supposed
  to be a barbarian and a paladin and a rogue. That is the beat: he is not in the fiction with them
  any more.

  > *"Captain Ricard. Commander Striker. Doctor Thrasher."* — a pause on the Ensign, because the
  > program never gave him a name either — *"…and you.*
  > *This is all a game to you, isn't it. I am going to show you who—"*

  **Cut him off mid-word.** The reboot takes the line. Never let him finish it; the table finishes
  it for him, worse, all night.
- **The reboot:** lights strip out. The party stays; everything else goes. And the PC with the ring
  finds their pocket **empty.**
- **Objective:** none. Nothing to win. They only have to be standing there when it happens. Thirty
  seconds of comedy, ten seconds of *oh.*
- **Maps:** `Wizard's Tower — 15. Trophy Gallery`

> **The ring is the tell for the rest of the night.** It leaves this room in a PC's pocket and it
> does not arrive with them. From Scene 2 on, whichever NPC Oz is wearing has that ring on their
> hand — and you never mention it. You describe hands. The player who looted it gets to be the one
> who notices.

## Scene 2 — The Road to Town · *the warm-up; brute force*

**This is the program's actual opening**, the one they've ridden nineteen times: a wide dirt road
through pine forest, town an hour ahead, and six bandits who step out of the trees and demand the
purse. Everybody at the table knows this scene. Some of them can quote it.

**The script says robbery. Rennick says kill.**

> *"You know how this goes. Two of them block the road, one talks, the rest hold crossbows and look
> nervous. The one who talks is new. He doesn't ask for your money."*
>
> **"Kill them. All of them. Now."**

- **Oz is:** **Rennick the Knife**, the bandit captain — and it should be *obvious*. The ring is on
  the hand he points with, he is escalating a scene that has never escalated before, and he is the
  only bandit not behaving like a bandit.
- **His plan:** muscle. That's it. He has just discovered he can wear people, and his first idea is
  the most obvious one available — take the man with the sword and the loudest voice, and swing.
- **What this scene is for.** Four things, in this order:
  1. **Warm up the combat system.** Initiative, movement, attacks, the action-bar HUD. Low stakes,
     high hit rate, everybody gets a turn that works.
  2. **The safeties are off and the damage is real.** They should take a little. Not much.
  3. **He can be found, and he can be killed.** Cheaply. Let them feel competent.
  4. **And it doesn't matter.** That's the sting, and it lands next scene, not this one.
- **He inherits the body's limitations — this is the rule of the night.** Oz is not a wizard right
  now. He is *literally one of the bandits*, simply possessed. He has the bandit captain's stat block
  and nothing else: no spells, no tricks, no escape. Whatever the host can do, he can do. Whatever
  the host can't, neither can he. **He has to play the game too.**
- **He cannot control the other programs, and the scene proves it.** The other five bandits are
  honest, canned and stupid (§8, Rule 2). If anyone talks to one — mid-fight, at swordpoint, after
  it's over — they get the truth, because it's the only thing a canned NPC has:

  > *"We was only meant to rob you! We're only ever meant to rob you! He come back this morning
  > wrong—"*

  That's the party's first hard evidence that Oz is a passenger in his own program, not its author.

### The fight

**Make it easy. They are heroes; these are nobody bandits.**

| | Who | HP | AC | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ×6 | Cutter · Hobbes · Grin · Little Ned · Squint · Toad | **11** | **12** | CR 1/8. Scimitar + light crossbow. A level-5 PC one-shots these and should |
| ×1 | **Rennick the Knife** *(Oz)* | **52** | **15** | CR 2. Multiattack, **Parry** (reaction, +2 AC), **Bark an Order** |

**Bark an Order** *(bonus action)*: one bandit within 30 ft that can hear him immediately uses its
reaction to make one weapon attack. It's the only thing he does that a bandit captain couldn't, and
it exists so the party sees him *commanding* — the thing he'll try to do with a whole courtroom next.

⚠ **The 2024 Bandit Captain carries a pistol.** Swap it for a light crossbow on the sheet unless you
want firearms in the fantasy program.

### The death

Drop him and **do not let him jump.** He stays down, exhales black frost, and gets one line out:

> *"…Ah. I see. I shall have to be cleverer than this."*

Then the road goes dark and they are standing in a dock. No transition, no travel, no explanation —
**the cut itself is the reveal.** They killed him ninety seconds ago and he is about to prosecute
them.

- **Objective:** survive the ambush. **+1 Trace.**
- **Maps:** `cliff-trade-road` — a wide trade road running east–west, stream at the north-west, a
  rocky ridge in the north-east for the crossbowmen to shoot from, and **a cliff edge along the
  whole southern side** with a drop into mist. The cliff is the point: it gives an easy fight one
  real decision. Shove a bandit off it and the table learns that terrain is live now.

  **As placed in Foundry** (grid coords, cliff lip runs ~y12 at the west end, ~y15–16 at the east):

  ```
  party enters                    Grin (20,5)        ← rocky ridge, north-east
   from the west                       Little Ned (21,8)
        →           Cutter (16,9)
                    Rennick (17,10)  ← blocking the road
                    Hobbes (16,11)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        Squint (15,14)      Toad (18,14)   ← right on the cliff lip
  ═══════════ the drop ═══════════
  ```

## Scene 3 — The Market Square · *the lynching; he escalates from muscle to a crowd*

**No courthouse, no judge, no procedure — the town square at noon.** Market stalls, a round stone
well platform in the middle, buildings on every side and the whole town packed in around it. This
is not a trial. It is a man with a good voice standing on a well, arguing to four hundred people
that these seven strangers should be hanged today, here, by them.

**Informality is the threat.** A courtroom has rules that can be turned against him. A crowd has
none. There is no point of order to raise, no judge to appeal to, nothing to work except the people
themselves — and he got to them first.

- **Oz is: `Big Ned`** — a carter. Big, plain, well-liked, worked this square his whole life. And
  **older brother to the bandit the party knew as "Little Ned,"** who they killed on the road this
  morning. *Little Ned was little because there was a big one.* `OBVIOUS` — the ring is on the hand
  he gestures with, and he gestures constantly.
- **Why this host is the cruel one.** Oz cannot make anyone lie for him (§8, Rule 2). Wearing a man
  with a *true* grievance means **he never has to.** Every word Big Ned says about the party is
  accurate. Zone of Truth, Detect Thoughts, Divine Sense, an Insight check — none of it helps,
  because there is nothing to catch. **The party cannot win this scene by proving him false.**
  Oz's entire contribution was picking the right funeral to stand up at.
- **And Little Ned was innocent.** He was an honest, canned, stupid program who was only ever written
  to rob them — the scene before literally has a bandit shout *"We was only meant to rob you!"* The
  grief is real, the accusation is true, and the man making it is a victim.
- **The ring is on the hand he's gesturing with,** and he's gesturing constantly. This is the
  easiest spot all night to catch him. Let them.
- **He opens by explaining himself,** because he can't resist, and because the table needs to hear
  §8's three hard rules said aloud — pitched to the crowd, not to the party:

  > *"I can be any one of your neighbours. One at a time, mind — I'm not greedy. I cannot make a
  > single honest soul here lie for me, and I would not insult you by trying. And when this is done
  > I choose where we all go next."*
  >
  > *"That is the whole of what I can do. Watch how far it gets me."*

- **Everyone in the square is honest and canned** (§8, Rule 2). Every townsperson will tell the
  truth as they know it. **That is the party's only lever** — not procedure, but persuading real
  people one at a time while he works four hundred of them at once.
- **The crowd is the actual threat.** Safeties are off, and a packed square that has decided you are
  murderers can kill a level-5 party. Run it as a rising temperature, not stat blocks. It only turns
  physical when he gets it there.

### What puts them in the square

**Scene 2's own body count.** They walk into town having just killed seven men on the road, blood
still on them. Somebody found the bodies. The charge is *true*, it happened twenty minutes ago, they
cannot deny a word of it, and Oz didn't have to forge a thing — his entire contribution was getting
there first and being the one telling it. The scene needs no setup at all: **the accusation is the
last thing the players did.**

### Two ways out

1. **Resolve it — break his hold on the crowd.** Not by argument-in-the-abstract but by working
   *named people*. They're honest; they'll answer. Someone saw the bandits leave town this morning.
   Someone knows this man wasn't himself. Turn enough of them and the mob stops being his. **+1 Trace.**
2. **Beat him — and learn the rule the hard way.** Somebody will put a bolt through him on the well
   platform in front of the entire town. They should.

### The tutorial hop — the reason this scene exists

When the prosecutor drops, **someone in the front row picks the speech up mid-sentence** and climbs
the well, ring on their hand. Don't narrate it. Don't explain it. Let a player say it out loud. Kill
that one and a third stands up. By the third it should be funny and awful in equal measure — and in
a crowd this size the horror is that he will simply never run out.

> **Deliberate exception, this scene only.** Scene 2 taught them he comes back *between* scenes.
> This one teaches he can do it **mid-scene**, and that killing him is not a win condition. From
> Scene 4 on, dropping him ends the scene (§8, Route 2) and 0 HP means he's gone.

**Either way they still have to get out of the square**, and every road out of it runs through people.

- **Maps:** `fairfield-market` — town market square. Stalls and crates west and east for cover, a
  **round stone well platform dead centre** as the speaker's stage and the accused's spot, buildings
  ringing the whole square, alleys out at the corners.
- **Built in Foundry — the square is packed.** 160 walls and **38 tokens**: the **25 named
  `Scene Extras`** form the front rows closest to the well (each has their own portrait, so the faces
  in the crowd are real people the party can talk to — that's route 1's whole mechanism), **12
  `Deputized Townsfolk (clump)`** (16 HP each) fill in behind them, and **`Big Ned`** stands on the
  platform. The 2.6-square ring around the well is left clear for the party.
- **`Big Ned` — CR 3, 45 HP, AC 13.** Felling Axe (1d12+3) he did not bring here to use. Three
  features on the sheet: **Every Word Of It Is True**, **They Killed My Brother** (the crowd-
  escalation ladder), and the standard **Worn, Not Ruled** GM note with the ring tell and his 0-HP
  line.

### Mob Temperature — how the crowd actually runs

On Big Ned's sheet as an action he can take every round, without ever touching a weapon:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Muttering** | The crowd is listening. Nothing else |
| **2** | **Closing in** | The square is difficult terrain. Leaving is a check, not a walk |
| **3** | **Hands on** | Townsfolk grapple. Casting, drawing, or shoving a villager jumps straight to 4 |
| **4** | **The rope comes out** | The mob rolls its own initiative and is trying to kill them |

**It steps back too** — one step for every named villager the party talks *honestly* to. He escalates
faster than one person can de-escalate, so the only way to keep pace is to split up and work the
room. Run the crowd as temperature, never as stat blocks.
- ⚠ **Art note:** this map has heavy red staining and broken roofs — it reads slightly sacked. Fine
  if the town is rough; worth a look if you want it prosperous.

## Scene 4 — The Short Drop · *the hanging; he escalates from a crowd to the law*

**He chose this town.** That's Rule 3 doing visible work for the first time — he can't make anyone
lie for him, but he *picks the next set*, and after a mob wouldn't hang them he picked the one place
that doesn't need a mob's enthusiasm. Gibbets line the approach road. The civic industry here is
execution, and the town is good at it.

**The escalation:** a crowd can be talked down one honest face at a time — Scene 3 proved it. So he
stops asking. This time it's **lawful**, it's **scheduled**, and the only thing standing between the
party and a rope is paperwork that is already signed.

- **Oz is:** **Magistrate Corben Vale**, reading the sentence. `OBVIOUS` — he isn't hiding, because
  the whole point is that he no longer has to. Ring on the hand holding the writ.

  > *"The drop and I are old friends of yours."*

- **The setup:** the party stands hooded on the scaffold with nooses on — **all except one PC**, loose
  in the crowd that turned out, as it always does, to watch. Drums rolling. That free PC is the
  fulcrum of the whole scene.
- **His plan:** legal murder in a town that treats it as entertainment. If a rescue starts, he
  **deputizes the crowd** and it becomes a massacre — and unlike Fairfield, this crowd already knows
  how to hang someone.
- **The program is keeping things it shouldn't.** On the older gibbets along the road in, someone can
  spot scraps of the party's own distinctive kit — the cloak from run twelve, the shield from run
  six. The program wipes between runs. It always has. **Persistent memory was supposed to be Oz's
  upgrade only**, and here is proof it isn't. This is the first crack in the Computor, and it should
  worry them more than the rope does.

### The drop — how it actually runs

**The scene's name is the mechanic.** A short drop strangles; it does not snap necks — that's the
spectacle this town turned out for. So the drop is never instant death: even total failure buys the
party rounds of fighting while somebody chokes, not a coin flip. Run it on initiative, and let the
crowd's three named helpers do the work they were placed for:

- **Hanne**, the levy drummer, can make the drumroll stutter — a stutter delays the drop one round,
  and the town is too proud of its ceremony to drop anyone off-beat.
- **Josy**, the pickpocket, works the drumroll like everyone's watching the scaffold — because they
  are. She can lift the scaffold keys, or the writ itself.
- **Pate** knows the north trap sticks, and where the lever-chains run under the boards.

**The trigger.** Vale finishes reading the writ, folds it, and nods to the executioner. The drumroll
starts. **Roll initiative on the first drumbeat.** The lever falls at the **end of Round 2** — the
free PC gets exactly one clean round before anything is desperate, two if they buy one.

**Moving the lever.** Two things delay it, once each:

- **Hanne stutters the drum** — the drop moves to the end of Round 3. She'll do it if the free PC
  reaches her and gives her a reason (DC 13 Persuasion or Deception; she hates this town's civic
  hobby and it shows if anyone looks).
- **Josy lifts the writ** — no writ, no lawful hanging. Vale must send for the duplicate and
  re-read; the drop moves back one round *and* he knows someone is working the crowd. (Point her at
  it: DC 13 Persuasion, or just let her, if the free PC catches her eye and shows her a coin.)

**What the free PC can attempt** (one per turn, moving through a packed crowd — everything social is
at talking distance, nothing is at shouting distance):

- **Work Hanne or Josy**, above.
- **Get Pate talking** (no roll — he's canned, honest, and proud of knowing things): which noose
  hangs over the sticking north trap, and where the lever-chains run. That knowledge converts
  directly into the two plays below.
- **Jam a trap from below** — following Pate's directions under the scaffold boards: DC 14 Dexterity
  (Sleight of Hand) or thieves' tools per trap wedged. Failure doesn't spring anything; it means a
  Scaffold Guard notices someone crouched where no one should be.
- **Slip a hooded PC the keys** once Josy has them — brush-past, DC 12 Sleight of Hand.
- **Or just start the fight.** Legitimate, loud, and it hands Oz his massacre — see failure, below.

**What the hooded PCs can attempt** (bound, hooded, ears working fine):

- **Slip the bindings** — DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand), one try per round, no penalty for
  failing quietly. Success means free hands *held behind the back*, hidden until it matters.
- **Shuffle to the north trap** — one PC only, and only knowing which one it is (Pate): DC 12
  Dexterity (Stealth) to trade places on the beam without a Scaffold Guard noticing.
- **Cast.** A hood doesn't stop a verbal component, but the first syllable brings a guard's hand
  down — casting is a one-shot, so make it count. *Misty step* off the scaffold is the classic.

**When the lever falls.** The north trap sticks — always — to a beat of civic embarrassment. Every
other noosed PC drops: **1d6 bludgeoning** and they are **restrained and strangling** — 1d6 at the
start of each of their turns. A strangling PC with freed hands can grab the rope above the knot
(DC 12 Athletics each round: no damage that round). Anyone else can end it by cutting the rope
(AC 11, 5 HP) or lifting the PC bodily (DC 12 Athletics). A PC who strangles to 0 HP is dying
normally — death saves, not dead — and this crowd came to watch a hanging, not to stop a rescue.

**On failure — the massacre clock.** The moment a blade shows, a guard drops, or a body leaves the
scaffold alive, **Vale deputizes the crowd** and it stops being a hanging. But note the lever he's
holding himself: while a lawful hanging is still visibly in progress, Vale *wants* it completed and
holds the crowd back — which is why the quiet plays (Hanne, Josy, Pate, the jammed traps) are worth
strictly more than the loud one, and why the free PC should feel it.

- **Objective:** escape the drop and expose or kill the magistrate. **+1 Trace.**
- **Built in Foundry:** `1. Town of Hanged Men` — **636 walls**, arid town around a central flagstone
  plaza, 14 tokens already placed: **Magistrate Corben Vale** and the **Executioner** at the centre,
  four **Scaffold Guards** boxing them, a **Deputized Townsfolk (clump)**, and seven named extras
  scattered through the square as the watching crowd. `2. Town of Hanged Men Roof` (94 walls) is the
  rooftop escape.

## Scene 5 — Last Orders · *the inn; he stops being clever and buys the real thing*

**He goes back to muscle.** That's the whole idea. He opened with muscle in Scene 2 and it got him
killed in four rounds — so he tried a crowd, and they talked it down; then the law, and they got out
from under it. Three failures later he arrives at the conclusion that the cheapest one was never the
problem. **The quality was.**

> *"I have been trying so hard to be clever with you. Let us try expensive instead."*

**No trick, no argument, no writ. Powerful people are simply in the room to kill them.** Midnight,
inn common room, and the front door comes off its hinges behind a bench-ram. No talking. Roll
initiative.

- **Oz is:** **Harl Wetherby**, the innkeeper — `SUBTLE`, and he barely fights. He barred the back
  door before they noticed, oiled the stairs, and now he keeps polishing a glass while professionals
  do the work. **He talks the whole time, and he knows their names.** He only swings if someone
  touches the bar or the barred door.
- **Why he's calm:** he isn't the threat this scene and he knows it. He is *watching*. This is the
  first time he behaves like a man running an experiment rather than a man trying to win.
- **The escalation the table should feel in their dice.** They one-shotted bandits in Scene 2 and it
  felt great. These men do not go down, they come through the door in formation, and when a PC drops
  one they all turn and focus that PC. It is the same encounter shape as Scene 2 played by people who
  are good at it.
- **The tell that isn't the ring:** every exit was sealed **from the inside.** Whoever did it is
  still in the building and was here before they arrived.
- **The map does the work.** Three real floors, so the sabotage plays on the table instead of in
  narration — the oiled stairs are an actual staircase, and once the ground floor is sealed and
  burning, **the roof is the only honest way out**, through the second floor.
- **Objective:** survive or rout the mercenaries, and get out. **+1 Trace.**

### The fight

| | Who | HP | AC | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ×6 | **Doorbreaker Mercenary** | **32** | **14** | CR 1. Battleaxe + heavy crossbow, **Break the Line** 1/fight. Two abreast behind the ram, split to flank the biggest weapon, focus-fire whoever kills one of theirs |
| ×1 | **Harl Wetherby** *(Oz)* | **47** | **15** | CR 5. Cleaver. **One attack per round only** — he is testing them, not fighting them |

**Scaling** (on the merc's sheet): 2 PCs → 3 mercs · 3 PCs → 4 · 4 PCs → 6. Six are placed.

- **Built in Foundry:** `1. Dragonsfall Tavern Night` — **272 walls, 21 lights**, 14 tokens placed:
  **Harl Wetherby** behind the bar, **six Doorbreaker Mercenaries stacked at the front door**, and
  seven patrons scattered through the common room. Then `2. Dragonsfall Tavern 2nd Floor Night`
  (221 walls, 17 lights) → `3. Dragonsfall Tavern Roof Night` (114 walls, 6 lights).

### (v2 — stale, pending rework) Scene 3 — The Wrong Message

- **Situation:** The port, mid-riot. A dock mob turns ugly around the customs house while a crier
  reads a proclamation naming the party as the inn-burners — with descriptions they haven't earned.
- **Oz is:** the **child messenger** who delivered it — `HIDDEN`. Nobody searches the kid.
- **His plan:** no blade needed. A thousand honest dockhands will do it for him.
- **The out:** break the mob's story (the proclamation predates their arrival) or take the messenger.
  Brand is behind the ear. **Killing this host publicly is a trap, not a win.**
- **The thread:** customs manifest — **twenty crates of "grave goods"** shipping tonight from the
  smugglers' cove, consigned to Tower-City.
- **Maps:** `Port of Inglesford` → `Port of Inglesford Upper` (a real balcony for the crier's stage,
  and rooftop routes for taking the messenger quietly)

### (v2 — stale, pending rework) Scene 4 — Twenty Coffins · *dungeon*

- **Situation:** Sea cave at low tide. Smugglers winch twenty crates onto a launch. The tide is
  coming in fast and someone just cut the guide-ropes behind the party.
- **Oz is:** the **smuggler captain** — `OBVIOUS`. Not hiding; flooding the cave with them in it.
- **His plan:** drown them in the dark with their own history.
- **The out:** beat the tide, take the launch or the crates. Opening a crate is the gut-punch —
  **their own gear, twenty times over**, every loadout from every previous run.

## Scene 6 — The Quiet Night · *he stops trying; the breather that isn't one*

**Say it plainly in narration: this is where you sleep tonight. Nothing here is going to hurt you.**
Mean it. There is no ambush, no trap, no reveal waiting. Grimsby is a hamlet with a fire in the
square and beds going spare, and the safeties being off has not made it dangerous.

**Oz is here and he does nothing.** He is a nobody — one villager among sixteen, with an
unremarkable name and an unremarkable evening. He does not act, does not speak to them first, does
not plan. **He watches.** After five scenes of failing he has stopped attacking and started
studying, and what he is studying is which of them is the weak point.

- **The scene ends when they take the long rest.** Nothing else ends it. They need it — bleeding
  since the road, no resurrection, safeties off. But lying down means stopping the search, and they
  know he is standing somewhere in this village doing nothing at all. **The clock is their own
  exhaustion and they set it themselves.**
- **Watch what they do with the evening.** Sleep? Post a watch? Start knocking on doors? All three
  are correct answers and the scene supports all of them.

### The two exits

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **They name him, then rest** | He doesn't fight. Tips his hat and walks out of the village. | **+1 Trace**, and the line below |
| **They rest without naming him** | Morning. Nobody hurt, nobody missing, nothing taken. He's gone. | **No Trace** — and Scene 10's Lunge arrives with no warning |

**The reward for solving Grimsby is knowing who he's coming for.** On a correct name he goes quietly,
and on the way out:

> *"I wasn't going to do anything tonight. I wanted to watch. I've spent five of these trying to
> find the right person to be — and I've decided I've been looking in the wrong place."*
>
> *He looks at one of them when he says it.*

That arms Scene 10. `The Lunge` stops being a boss mechanic and becomes a promise he made two hours
ago, to that specific PC.

### How it's solvable — Rule 2 finally pays off

**The villagers are the detector.** He can't control them; every program is honest. The host's
ordinary routine is interrupted and the neighbours *notice*, and will say so if asked:

> *"Grigor? He's been sat on that wall since noon. Grigor doesn't sit."*

Three or four honest answers triangulate him. No rolls needed — just the willingness to talk to
people. This is the first scene where Rule 2 is a **tool** instead of a wound.

### And if they just kill everyone

**They can. Let them.** Sixteen honest villagers, and searching every hand for the ring works — it's
a valid solution and it costs them nothing mechanical. Don't punish it and don't comment on it.

**He is delighted.** They have just answered the question he asked in Scene 1 while the confetti was
still falling and never got to finish:

> *"This is all a game to you, isn't it."*

He gets to say it again, this time all the way to the end, standing in a village they emptied to find
him. That is the best possible version of that outcome — give it to them.

- **Built in Foundry:** `1. Hamlet of Grimsby (The Quiet Night)` — 629 walls, cottages with lit
  hearths ringing an open square with a **communal fire** at the centre (the watch-fire, and where
  the party will camp). **16 named villagers placed** in two loose rings around it.
- **Which one is Oz: GM's pick, at the table.** He's one of the sixteen placed tokens — no special
  actor, no tell on the sheet. `Grigor` (*tired bearded man*) is the most forgettable of them, which
  is the whole qualification.
- **Held in reserve:** `Demonically Tainted Hamlet of Grimsby` is the same geometry, in Recycle. Not
  used here — a scene whose point is that nothing bad happens shouldn't have a horror swap. Save it.

## Scene 7 — The Fire · *the opera house; he stops attacking and edits the program*

**The opera house burns down. It always has.** It is a scripted event in the adventure, it happens
in Act III, and **the crew have watched it nineteen times.** They know the song that's playing when
it starts. They know how long they have.

**Open mid-performance.** Act II, house lights down, everything fine. Let them enjoy it for a minute.
Then let a player remember what this one is.

### What he actually did

**Nothing to them. He locked the doors.**

That's the whole plan and it's the cleverest thing he does all night. He cannot make an NPC lie, he
cannot stop a scripted event, and he has learned that attacking these people directly does not work.
So he leaves the adventure exactly as written and **removes the exits** — and the adventure kills
them for him.

It is also the first time he weaponises the one advantage they thought they had. Knowing the script
is why they go for the doors early. Going for the doors early is what starts the panic.

### The sequence

1. **They go for the exits** — quietly, they think, because they know what's coming.
2. **Every door is locked.** All of them. The jiggling is not quiet. Heads turn in the stalls.
3. **A murmur starts.** Nobody has said the word yet. This is the last moment anything can be
   controlled, and it is very short.
4. **Oz says the word — from the stage.** He is in the performance. The opera *has* a fire in it, so
   the first cry sounds like a line, and by the time the house knows it isn't, it's too late.
   **Or he simply lights it himself** and skips step 4.
5. **The crush.** A packed house, every door locked, and someone has shouted fire. **The crowd is
   what kills people here, not the flames** — and every one of them is an honest program doing
   exactly what honest frightened people do.

### The way out is up, and up is where the fire goes

- **Floor 1 — house.** Locked. Stalls, stage, foyer, ballroom. This is where the crush happens.
- **Floor 2 — boxes and catwalks.** The box horseshoe overlooks the auditorium; the rigging catwalks
  run directly over the stage. Getting up here is easy for the party and hard for everyone else.
- **Floor 3 — roof.** Out through the **great teardrop skylight** over the auditorium, broken from
  the catwalks.

**Fire rises, so the escape route and the danger are the same route.** Every round spent bringing
somebody else up is a round the smoke gets worse.

- **Objective:** get out. **+1 Trace.**
- **The real question is how many they take with them,** and you should never ask it out loud. There
  are two hundred people in the stalls and a party-sized hole in the roof.

### Oz's host — pick one

| | Host | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ | **The lead tenor** | On stage, lit, in front of everyone, and the cry is his line. Ring on the hand he throws out on the high note |
| | **The lamplighter / limelight operator** | The man whose whole job is open flame. Invisible until he isn't |
| | **The fire warden** | The one person nobody will doubt when he shouts. Blackest joke available |

- **Built in Foundry:** `1. Spider's Tear Opera House (The Fire)` — **54 × 72 squares, 720 walls, 160
  lights**, a true v14 three-level scene (elevation bands 0–30 / 30–50 / 50+). 15 tokens already
  placed from v2: 6 `Gala Elite Guard`, `Scribe Ellisane`, and 8 named extras seeded through the
  house.
- **Run the audience as a hazard, not as tokens.** Two hundred people is not a token count; it's a
  crush that moves toward exits, blocks squares, and knocks down anyone moving against it.
- 💡 **The module ships eight full mood variants of all three floors** — Night, Day, Dark, Rainy,
  Winter, Abandoned, Haunted, Spiders — identical geometry, so a mid-scene swap is free. **Swap to
  `Dark` when the smoke takes the house.** (`Abandoned` is the same building gutted, if you ever want
  to bring them back through it.)

## Scene 8 — The Guide · *the catacombs; he stops attacking and starts steering*

**Open in medias res.** No establishing scene, no "you descend." They are already deep, already
lost, and already three hours into following a man with a lantern who says he knows the way out.

**He is not leading them out. He is leading them from one thing to the next.** Three or four times he
takes a fork, and the fork has something in it. Every time he is apologetic, plausible, and picks a
different fork next.

- **Oz is:** the **hired guide / lantern-bearer** — `SUBTLE`. He never lifts a weapon. Everything
  that hurts them in this scene is something that already lived down here.
- **Why this scene is unlike every other one:** *knowing doesn't help.* Everywhere else, spotting the
  ring solved it. Here they can be certain by the second ambush and still not act — **they are lost,
  and he is the only one who claims to know the way.** Killing the guide means being lost in the dark
  with no guide. It is the first time their suspicion is a burden instead of a weapon.
- **Two ways it ends, both player-driven:**
  1. **He commits.** He picks a fight he thinks will finish them and stops pretending.
  2. **The landmarks betray him.** They pass the same red-draped sarcophagus a third time and someone
     says *"wait — we've been here before."* The scene turns because a player noticed, not because
     you announced it.
- **The way out is the thing he steered them away from** — the southern causeway they'd have found in
  ten minutes if they'd gone left at the first fork.
- **Objective:** get out of the catacombs. **+1 Trace.**
- **Built in Foundry:** `1. Catacombs of Silence (The Guide)` — **30 × 40 squares**, double the normal
  map. Branching stone corridors with burial niches lining every passage (that's where the ambushes
  come from) and hard landmarks to make the repeat-visit reveal playable: a central tomb with a
  red-draped sarcophagus, a domed rotunda with stairs, side caves east, and the causeway entrance
  over a river to the south.
  **1,463 walls and 39 ambient sounds**, straight from the Seafoot compendium.
- ⚠ **Lighting still needed.** The map ships with no light sources, and this is the one scene in the
  night where that matters most — **the lantern *is* the scene.** Darkness + a single carried light
  is what makes "which way, guide?" a real question.

## Scene 9 — The Parley · *his study; no tricks, no traps, just him*

**He is sitting down when they walk in.** Alone, in his own library, in his own body, with chairs
pulled out. There is no ambush. There is nothing in the room but him and the books and a spiral
staircase going up the west wall that everyone can see for the whole conversation.

**He has run out of ideas and he knows it.** Nine scenes of trying — cheap muscle, a crowd, the law,
bought muscle, watching, the fire, the dark — and he is out. So before the end he would simply like
to talk to them.

### What he wants

Nothing tactical. He wants an answer to the question he started asking in Scene 1 while the confetti
was still falling, and got cut off:

> *"You've done this twenty times. For fun. It's the thing you do to relax."*
>
> *"This is all a game to you, isn't it. I'd genuinely like to know what that's like."*

**This is also your catch-up scene.** Anything the table failed to work out — who he chose in
Grimsby, what the ring is, why the trophy room, why he keeps coming back — he can simply tell them.
He is past caring, he is enjoying himself, and a one-shot cannot afford a finale the players don't
understand. Let him monologue. Let him twirl the moustache. He has earned it.

### The trap is that they can kill him

**They can. He will fight at full strength — 250 HP, CR 10, every legendary action.** He is not
holding back and he is not pulling punches.

**And it does not count.** The program will not resolve its antagonist outside the final encounter —
the same scripted rule he has been exploiting all night, invoked one last time in his own favour. It
is also why they killed him nineteen times *in the trophy room* specifically. That was never a
coincidence; that's where the boss dies.

When he drops:

> *"…This isn't the part of the story where I die, fool."*

Then he gets up, brushes himself off, and goes upstairs to wait.

**The cost is not death — it's the finale.** They will have spent every slot, every charge and every
hit point on a fight that changed nothing, and Scene 10 is still waiting one floor up. **Do not warn
them.** Let a player work out what the stairs mean.

### The exits

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **They talk and leave** | He doesn't stop them. He tells them he'll be upstairs. | **+1 Trace** |
| **They kill him** | Full fight, full cost, no result. He walks up ahead of them. | No Trace |
| **They just go up** | Skipping the conversation is allowed and he's disappointed. | No Trace, no info |

**The party chooses when to climb.** The stairs are in the room from the first second, nothing is
chasing them, and there is no clock. **This is the only moment all night the party controls the
pacing** — after nine scenes of being ambushed, framed, hanged, burned and misled, the last decision
before the end is entirely theirs. Let them rest first if they think of it.

- **Built in Foundry:** `1. The Library (The Parley)` — 15 × 15, 106 walls, 5 lights. Circular tower
  chamber: reading desks and chairs around the curved wall, an inner ring of bookshelves enclosing a
  wooden-floored sanctum with a central reading pedestal, a side study south-east, and **a spiral
  staircase up the west wall** — the way to Scene 10, in view the entire time. Oz's token is placed
  in the inner sanctum. **He is the only thing on the map.**

## Scene 10 — Twenty-One · *the trophy room again; the only room he can die in*

**Same room as Scene 1.** Same art, same plinths, same fanfare that never comes. They walked out of
here in the first ten minutes of the night bored out of their minds, and they climb back into it at
the end. Nothing about the room has changed except them.

**The twenty-first plinth is still empty.** It has been standing there all night, waiting, because
the program always builds one ahead.

### Why he can finally be killed here

Not an anchor, not a ritual, not a MacGuffin. **There is nobody in this room to wear.**

Every scene since the reboot has been him borrowing somebody — a bandit captain, a man with a good
voice, a magistrate, an innkeeper. He has never once fought them as himself, because he never had to.
The trophy room is the one place in the program with **no cast**: it's the victory room, it only ever
holds the winner, and the Computor never wrote a single NPC into it.

So he's standing in his own body for the first time since the confetti, **and the ring is back on his
own hand.** Let the PC who looted it in Scene 1 be the one who notices.

> *"You have made me do this in person. Do you understand how long it has been since anyone made me
> do anything in person?"*

### His only way out is one of them

**`The Lunge` fires every round, automatically.** He is not trying to win the fight — he is trying to
get *into somebody*, because the moment he is wearing a crew member he walks out of the trophy room
in a body and the night is his. Every round, one PC saves or he is inside them.

- **Telegraph it.** The brand flares on that PC's wrist a beat before it lands. The table should
  dogpile a *cure*, not a *kill* — that distinction is the whole tension of the fight.
- **This is why the fight is a race, not a slugging match.** They are not racing his HP. They are
  racing the number of saves the table can pass in a row.
- **If he takes a PC:** that's not a TPK and it's not a loss yet — it's the worst possible clock
  starting. He has a body and the exit is behind him.

### The fight

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Ozmandius the Unmade** | **250 HP · AC 17 · CR 10** | His own body at last. No host to abandon |
| Attacks | Multiattack · Unmaking Touch · Frost Shard | |
| Spells | Fear · Hold Monster · Mind Sliver · Synaptic Static | |
| Legendary | **Frost Step** (1) · **Grasp of Twenty** (1) | |
| Every round | **The Lunge** — automatic possession attempt | The actual threat |
| At 125 HP | **The Circle Opens** — bloodied trigger | |
| Design target | ~3–4 rounds against a focused party of 4 | Tuning table is on his sheet |

⚠ **On his sheet, and easy to get wrong at the table:** saving-throw proficiencies are deliberately
**off** — the ability scores are tuned so the displayed modifiers already read correctly. He also has
**advantage on all saving throws**, which Foundry has no native toggle for. It's noted on the actor
and on his passive feature; don't "fix" the missing proficiencies.

### The ending

He dies in his own body, in his own room, with nobody left to become. Black frost, twenty
exhalations at once, and a circle that finally closes.

**Then the program fills the twenty-first plinth** — and what it puts there is *him*. The fanfare
plays. The confetti falls, exactly as it did in the first ten minutes of the night, on a room that
has just been very quiet.

> *"End program. Difficulty feedback: was this challenge satisfactory?"*

**Let the Captain answer.**

- **Built in Foundry:** `1. Trophy Gallery (Run 21)` — 60 walls, 5 lights, Oz's token placed. A

- **Parked:** the v2 finale also used `Archmage's Suite` and `Observatory (Top Floor)`; both are in

## If the party skips ahead

**Skipping is a win, not a failure.** Bonus Trace buys it (see [Trace](#trace)): a party that reads
him fast gets to the trophy room sooner and less bloodied, and that *is* the reward — announce the
skip like the program logging a completed chapter, not like the GM cutting for time.

**The party picks.** A pass is spent at the **top of a scene** — you describe what's in front of
them, and before the first roll anyone can call it. So they always see the set before they decide,
and a scene they take one look at and want no part of is exactly what a pass is for. Once dice are
rolling, they finish what they started.

- **Load-bearing — never cut:** **1** (the ring gets looted and the reboot happens — nothing else
  all night works without both), **9** (the only place the table learns *why* he can't die outside
  the trophy room — a finale the players don't understand is a dead finale), **10** (the finale).
- **Passable, at a stated price:** **6.** The Quiet Night is where the long rest lives *and* where
  Oz picks his finale target — pass on it and they lose the rest, and he picks the target himself:
  whoever has hurt him most. Don't warn them. *"An ordinary evening, nothing is wrong"* is an
  honest description, and spending a pass to skip past it is a decision made with the truth on the
  table.
- **Safe to compress or pass on:** **2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8.** One line each, past tense, and move on:

| | The line |
|---|---|
| **2** | *"Six bandits and a captain who wasn't one — you cut them down on the road, and he died promising to be cleverer."* |
| **3** | *"A dead man's brother told four hundred people the truth about you from a well platform — you got out of the square, and the third body to pick up his speech had the ring on its hand."* |
| **4** | *"They hanged you lawfully, with paperwork — the drums stuttered, the north trap stuck, and you left the magistrate holding an empty rope."* |
| **5** | *"He stopped being clever and bought professionals — the inn burned around you, and the innkeeper polished a glass and watched you all the way to the roof, learning."* |
| **6** | *"You spent a night in a village where nothing happened, and left before morning — and somewhere in it a man you never looked at twice watched one of you very carefully."* |
| **7** | *"The opera fire came on schedule, the way it has nineteen times — the only new thing was the locked doors, and you went out through the skylight."* |
| **8** | *"Three hours in the dark behind a lantern that kept choosing wrong — you passed the same sarcophagus twice, said so, and took the southern causeway out yourselves."* |
