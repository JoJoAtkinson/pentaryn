# Space Journey — the eight pregens

Companion to [[oneshots/space-journey|space-journey.md]]. Eight level 5 D&D 2024 characters, pinned so
that **no choice is left on the sheet**. Built as Foundry actors in the `Space Journey — Pregens`
folder of the `space-journey` world.

> ⚠️ **Stale for two of the eight.** **Ballad Quinn** and **Pip Locksley** — the only two in play,
> for Kris and Kyle — were audited and rebuilt on 2026-08-16. Their entries below describe the
> *original* builds and are out of date: Ballad's prepared list no longer contains Heroism, and
> Pip's Cunning Strike is no longer pre-resolved to *Trip*. See
> [[space-journey-pc-tuneup|space-journey-pc-tuneup.md]] for their current sheets, the accuracy
> bugs that were fixed, and the DM rulings still outstanding. **The other six entries are accurate.**

> **The no-healing rule was cut.** Party healing, potions, Hit Dice, Second Wind and Lay on Hands
> all work as written — see §6 of the main doc. The audit that used to live here is kept below as a
> **historical note only**, because it explains two choices that outlived it: Sister Vell is a
> Warlock rather than a Life Cleric, and the pinned spell lists are light on healing. Both are still
> good calls — Vell is a better character and the lists are tighter — so neither was reverted.

## Historical note — the rule that used to be here

For most of this document's life, no player character could restore hit points to another player
character; the ship's E.M.H. was the entire healing economy. **That rule is gone.** It was elegant
and it was too much scaffolding for one night of comedy — it required reflavouring class features,
banning feats and gear, and re-deriving the damage budget, all to protect a metaphysical
justification nobody at the table was going to ask about twice.

What replaced it is one sentence: *hard light mends you the same way it cut you.*

Two decisions from that era were kept because they stand on their own merits, not on the rule:

- **Sister Vell is a Warlock (Fiend), not a Life Cleric.** She started as a class-replacement to
  dodge Disciple of Life. She ended up the best-realised character in the lineup — the nun whose
  prayers started getting answered by the wrong department — and Eldritch Blast is a far better
  Simple sheet than Preserve Life.
- **The pinned spell lists stay light on healing.** Not banned, just not front-loaded. A pinned list
  is there to stop a new player drowning in options; leaving out the backup-healbot pull keeps each
  sheet doing the one thing its hook promises. Add Cure Wounds if a table wants it.

The Fighter's *Adrenaline Surge* and the Paladin's *Bulwark Vow* were house-ruled replacements for
Second Wind and Lay on Hands. **Both are reverted** — those features are back to normal in the
Foundry build.

| **Sister Vell** — Warlock (Fiend) | Was originally a Life Cleric | See the historical note above. Kept on merit |
| **Magister Orin** — Wizard (Evoker) | Prepared list pinned; no Sculpt Spells until L6 | *"Your fireball does not have a friends setting."* |
| **Ballad Quinn** — Bard (Lore) | Prepared list pinned | Heroism gives temp HP every turn — the support that never stalls a turn |
| **Thessa Longsight** — Ranger (Hunter) | Prepared list pinned; **Archery** style required | Her pinned **+9** longbow depends on Archery's +2 |
| **Dame Corriel** — Paladin (Devotion) | None. Lay on Hands intact | Aura of Devotion is L6, so she has none at 5 — noted on the sheet |

**Two feats are missing from the 2024 SRD pack** (`dnd5e.feats24` has 17) and are authored as world
items in the Foundry build, flagged `space-journey.authored`: **Tough** (Skarn) and **Musician**
(Quinn). `Magic Initiate` also ships with no Cleric/Wizard/Druid variants, so the pinned spells are
added to the sheet directly.

## Pinned builds — no decisions left on the sheet

**Ability scores, pinned** — these are what make the attack strings below correct. The primary stat
is **16, not 18**: the pinned "+7" on a +1 weapon is 3 (prof) + 3 (stat) + 1 (magic), and an 18

### Bram Ironvow — Fighter (Champion) — MEDIUM
HP 44, AC 19 (chain mail + shield + **Defense** style). Human, Soldier (feat: **Savage Attacker**).
Longsword +1 (+7, 1d8+4; mastery **Sap**), 4 javelins (mastery **Slow**).
**Improved Critical** — crits on 19–20. **Remarkable Athlete** — Advantage on Initiative and
Athletics. **Action Surge** ×1. **Tactical Mind**, **Tactical Shift**.
**Second Wind** ×3 — Bonus Action, regain **1d10+5 HP**, back on a short rest.
> *Turn script:* "Attack twice. Big round? Action Surge, attack twice more. Hurt? Second Wind."
>
> **If you own the 2024 PHB, make him a Battle Master instead** — pinned maneuvers *Precision
> Attack*, *Trip Attack*, *Riposte*; 4 superiority dice (d8), back on a short rest. It's the better
> sheet. Champion is here only because Battle Master isn't in the SRD.

Cunning Action; Fast Hands; Second-Story Work.

### Sister Vell — Warlock (Fiend) — SIMPLE

### Magister Orin — Wizard (Evoker) — COMPLEX
HP 32, AC 12 (15 with Shield up). High elf, Sage (feat: **Magic Initiate — Wizard**, pinned:
*True Strike*, *Light*, *Find Familiar* — the owl is pre-statted).
Cantrips: Fire Bolt, Ray of Frost, Mage Hand, Prestidigitation. DC 14.
Prepared (all 9 pinned): **Magic Missile, Shield, Burning Hands, Misty Step, Scorching Ray, Web,
Fireball, Counterspell, Fly.** *Vampiric Touch deliberately excluded — it heals the caster.*
> **BOXED ON THE SHEET:** 2024 Evokers don't get Sculpt Spells until level 6.
> *"Your fireball does not have a friends setting. Aim accordingly."*
> And that's not just a gag anymore: death in this program is **permanent**, and 8d6 into a
> crewmate who's already bleeding is a funeral you cast yourself. A friendly-fire fireball can
> kill a fellow player character for real, forever, with no take-backs — the joke is the *aiming
> anxiety*, not the body.

This is the party's **only Counterspell** — and for nine scenes out of ten it has nothing to hit,
because Oz wears hosts who mostly can't cast. Bank it for the scene-10 finale, where he finally is
a wizard again — see the Oz interaction note below.

## Consistency, redundancy, swing

- **Complexity spread:** 3 Simple (Skarn, Pip, Vell), 3 Medium (Bram, Thessa, Corriel), 2 Complex
  (Orin, Quinn). Quinn needs your most attentive player (reaction timing); Orin your most careful
  one (friendly-fire fireball).
- **Action economy parity:** everyone has one attack-or-cast action, one meaningful bonus action,
  at most one reaction. No pets except Corriel's optional horse and Orin's owl — both flavor, tell
  players to ignore them under time pressure.
- **Redundancy:** none fatal. Vell and Quinn split the enchantment space deliberately (Vell: Hold
  Person / Fear; Quinn: Hypnotic Pattern / Suggestion / Slow). Bram, Skarn, and Corriel are all
  frontline but play nothing alike.
- **Swing check:** Fireball without Sculpt Spells is the swingiest thing here — a feature with a
  warning label. **Counterspell has almost nothing to bite on this year.** In scenes 1–9 Oz has
  only his host's stat block — a bandit captain, a magistrate, an innkeeper, a tenor — so for most
  of the night he simply isn't casting anything to counter. The spell only earns its slot in the
  scene-10 finale, where he's a CR 10 caster with legendary actions and Counterspell is suddenly
  the best card in the deck. Tell Orin's player this up front so the slot gets banked, not wasted
  probing innkeepers. Hold Person on a host just triggers a Scene Jump ("cornered") — working as
  intended.
- **Coverage gaps the E.M.H. must fill:** (1) all HP restoration, in and out of combat;
  (2) **condition removal** — nobody has Lesser/Greater Restoration anymore, so poison, paralysis,
  blindness, and disease have no party answer. The doctor's **"Fix them!"** order (ends one
  condition, once per crewmate per scene, separate from his heal) exists for exactly this reason;
  (3) getting downed PCs back up. **Nobody has Revivify and nothing in the game undoes a death**
  (Wharf's two Klingon exceptions aside) — the only answer to a dead player is the Redshirt
  engine (§5), which is why the GM should extend it to anyone who dies, not just the Redshirt:
  everyone's replacement arrives at the start of the next scene, and only the Redshirt's walks in
  mid-fight.

## Lethality math (why the doctor's budget is what it is)

Average pregen ~44 HP (32 on the wizard). Ten persistent scenes at ~12 real HP/PC/scene is
~90–100 HP of incoming per PC — 2–3× their maximum. Recovery has to come from somewhere, and it now
comes from three places instead of one. In priority order:

1. **The Doctor heals each crew member once per scene, 2d8+5 (~14 HP).** Ten scenes, so ~140 HP of
   recovery available per PC against ~90–100 incoming — but only if the party spends the round's
   order on healing instead of the distraction, which in a hot fight they often can't. That
   tension *is* the resource management; there is no counter to track.
2. **Cap scene damage at ~10–12 average real HP/PC/scene**, hazards telegraphed, save-for-half —
   just under what one heal undoes, so the party bleeds slowly all night rather than spiking.
   Scenes 2–4 run lighter (~8) by their own nature — a trivial bandit ambush, a lynching where the
   crowd is the threat, a hanging you escape rather than fight — so the first real fight is the
   scene-5 inn siege and nobody should be desperate walking into it.
3. **The two breathers — scene 5's quiet night at the inn (no combat at all) and scene 8's parley (no
   required fight) — restore everything but HP.** Pact slots, Bardic Inspiration, Channel
   Divinity and Second Wind come back; wounds don't. **Hit Dice are the one thing a breather never
   hands back** — that single restriction is what keeps nine scenes from going slack, and it's the
   one worth holding onto now that everything else about healing is unrestricted.
4. **Party healing is the third source** — Lay on Hands (25 HP), Second Wind ×3, plus any Cure
   Wounds a table decides to prepare. Together with the Doctor that's *generous*, and it should be:
   the real threats here are the hazards and the fact that **death is permanent**, not attrition.

Scaling is automatic: the heal is per crew member, so a 4-seat and a 7-seat table get the same
attrition curve. If a dry run runs hot, turn exactly one dial — **let the Doctor heal the same person
twice in a scene**. Don't add rest windows (they cost 10 minutes of a 3-hour clock) and don't add
rules to the doctor; his whole value is that he fits on an index card.
