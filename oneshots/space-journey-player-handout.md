# Space Journey — player handout

> You are the senior staff of the **U.S.S. Interprize, NCC-1701-Dee**. Off duty, you run a
> sword-and-sorcery holo-program called *The Tower of Oz*. You have beaten it twenty times. It is
> boring. Tonight someone said **"Computor. Make the wizard an actual challenge,"** and the Computor
> gave Oz the memory of all twenty deaths.
>
> He seized the scene engine, locked the arch, and shut off the safety parameters.
>
> **The blades are real now.**

**How you win:** earn **10 Trace**. Every scene you take off the board pays it, three ways —
resolve the scene's objective, find and kill Oz (which cuts the scene short), or break the set open
with one of your abilities. There are nine scenes and nine Trace, so grinding it out always gets you
there — but **every bonus Trace banks a free pass: one scene, of your choosing, that you simply
refuse to play.** You'll always see the set before you decide — the GM describes it, and any of you
can call the pass before the first roll. Walk into something you don't like the look of and you can
turn around and walk out, and that scene never gets to hurt you. (It buys blood and time, not
Trace: the scene you passed on never pays its +1.) Grind and you arrive bloodied; hunt well and you
arrive early. At
10 Trace he's cornered in the trophy room — the room where you've killed him nineteen times — and
*that* death is final.

**Killing him is the easy part. Running him out of rooms is the job.**

## Pick 1 — your post on the ship

This is your **meta layer**. It grants two holodeck abilities that work on the *program* rather than
the fiction, which is why they feel like cheating. They are supposed to feel like cheating.

| Ship role | Name | Once per **scene** | Once per **session** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Captain | **Capt. Jean-Luc Ricard** ★ | **Engage** — name one objective out loud; every crewmate who pursues it rolls with **advantage** | **Objection, Computor** — argue in character that the program is breaking its own rules and **overrule one GM ruling** |
| First Officer | **Cmdr. William T. Striker** ★ | **You're With Me** — name a crewmate; they immediately take a **full extra turn** | **Standard Issue** — he brought a phaser. One shot, full power, cannot miss |
| Android Ops | **Lt. Cmdr. Datum** ★ | **Positronic Recall** — ask one factual question about this scene; get a true answer | **Interface** — take control of one program. It obeys you literally and **stays with you until it dies** |
| Chief Engineer | **Lt. Cmdr. Jordy LaFarge** ★ | **Panel Glitch** — tear open a maintenance panel. Ends the scene outright and banks a free pass | **Reroute Power** — safeties back on; **nobody takes damage** for a full round |
| Security / Tactical | **Lt. Wharf, son of Mogg** ★ | *Passive, always on:* **Today Is a Good Day** — die where no crewmate can see it and it doesn't take | **He Prefers Not to Discuss It** — come back even when they watched. No explanation, and none is coming |
| Ship's Counselor | **Counselor Deanna Trois** *(say "twah")* | **I Sense Deception** — point at anyone. You learn **silently** whether they are Oz. No roll, no lie | **Deep Empath** — learn Oz's *goal* this scene, not just where he is |
| Chief Medical | **Dr. Beverly Thrasher** ★ | **"I'll State the Nature of the Emergency"** — cut the Doctor off and give him a **second order** this round | **Regulation 121, Section A** — relieve every program **in sight** of duty. Whoever is left standing is Oz |
| Conn / Boy Genius | **Acting Ensign Weasely Thrasher** ★ | **"Acting Ensign!"** — a crewmate puts you in your place; reroll a failed check **with Expertise** | **God, He's Annoying** — recreate any *other* role's once-per-scene ability, smugly |
| Bartender, Deck Nine Aft | **Gwynan** | **Just Listening** — chat with any NPC; advantage on your next roll **and** hand an ally Inspiration | **The Long Memory** — one true secret about this scene, no roll |
| Nobody | **Ensign** ★ | **Sacrificial Interpose** — take a hit aimed at an ally | **Statistically Inevitable** — when you die, your replacement walks in **immediately, mid-scene, into the same fight**. Everyone else's replacement waits for the next scene; his doesn't |

### ★ — there's fine print, and you should read it *after* you pick

A star means that ability has a full writeup with exact limits, and the one-line version above is
not the whole truth. Ask your GM for the long version once the role is yours — several of them have
a catch that decides how you play the whole night. Three examples, so you know the kind of thing:

- **Today Is a Good Day** only works if *no crewmate can see you die.* If they watch, you're dead.
- **Panel Glitch** needs a panel to have been spotted first, and **spotting one is never your
  choice** — the seams only show to someone who was looking at something else.
- **Regulation 121-A** reaches only what the Doctor **can see.** If Oz is round a corner, everyone
  in the room sits down, nobody is left standing, and you've spent it for one negative fact.

The two roles **without** a star — Counselor and Bartender — are the pick-up-and-play ones. What
you see is what they do.

## The Doctor — you run him, not the GM

**Nobody had to volunteer to be the healbot.** You get the ship's Emergency Medical Holograph, who
isn't a party member — he's a talking item that heals, and you run him yourselves. Your own healing
works normally on top of him. This card is his complete rules.

> ### THE DOCTOR — E.M.H. Mk-Won
>
> He is always with the party. **He cannot die. He cannot be hacked.**
>
> **No order → he hides.** He's a doctor, not a fighter.
>
> **Once per round, anyone may shout one order. He does exactly that, then hides again.**
>
> | Order | What happens |
> |---|---|
> | **"Heal me!"** | He runs to that crewmate and restores **2d8+5 HP**. Once per crewmate per scene |
> | **"Distract them!"** | He runs into the open shouting. One enemy within 30 ft **wastes its next attack on him** — it passes right through. Each enemy falls for this once per scene |
> | **"Fix them!"** | He runs to that crewmate and ends **one condition** — poisoned, paralyzed, blinded, frightened, whatever the scene stuck on them. Once per crewmate per scene, and it's a separate once from the healing |
>
> **Anything else** → *"I'm a doctor, not a [whatever you just asked for]."* He does not move.
>
> **Free and unlimited, out of combat: "Doctor, take a look at this."** He examines a body, a wound
> or a sick NPC and tells you **one true thing.** He is never wrong and he never speculates.
>
> **He is free healing nobody had to volunteer for.** Your own spells, potions and Hit Dice work
> normally on top of him.

**He mends programs too, free and unlimited.** In the fiction he's a cleric, so **"Heal that
guard!"** costs you nothing — it doesn't use up the round's order or anyone's once-per-scene. A
mended civilian walks to high ground instead of being carried. A patched soldier goes back on the
wall. Use him on the scene, not just on yourselves.

## Seven things that will kill you if nobody says them out loud

1. **0 HP means death saves, and death is real.** Nothing in this game undoes it — no resurrection,
   no crash cart. The one exception is a Klingon, twice: Wharf shrugs off any death no crewmate
   sees, and once a night he walks back in from one you *did* see. Neither the Computor nor anyone
   else can explain it, and he prefers not to discuss it. For the rest of you: die, and a crewman
   wanders in looking for the gym at the start of the **next scene**, and you're back on a fresh
   sheet. Nobody watches from the couch.
2. **Killing Oz is not progress.** He doesn't die; he jumps. It's worth Trace and it ends the
   scene, but the scene's *objective* is worth Trace too — and if you drop him early you lose the
   chance at it.
3. **He wears exactly one NPC per scene, and he only gets that body's abilities.** No lightning
   from the innkeeper's fingers — if the body can't do it, he can't do it. Corner the host and
   you've cornered the wizard.
4. **Look for the ring.** A signet ring: a circle cut into the band that stops just short of
   closing. It's on the hand of whoever he's wearing, and it is never on the body afterward.
   Hunting it is the game.
5. **He comes back at full HP every scene. You don't.** Your damage carries all night.
6. **Healing works; don't ask how.** Hard light mends you the same way it cut you. That's the whole
   answer. Prevention still beats healing, though — it also saves you the turn.
7. **Environmental hazards are the real threat** — the opera-house fire behind locked doors, the
   catacombs you are lost in, the six hired professionals coming through the inn door. Oz is a wall
   you learn to knock over. The scene is what actually gets people.
